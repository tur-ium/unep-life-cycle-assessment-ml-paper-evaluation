Okay, let's break down the climate change impact of 5 kg of CO2 versus 1 kg of N2O according to the EF 3.1 impact assessment method.  The key here is using **Global Warming Potential (GWP)**.

**Understanding Global Warming Potential (GWP)**

GWP is a relative measure of how much heat a greenhouse gas traps in the atmosphere compared to CO2 over a specific time horizon (usually 100 years).  CO2 is assigned a GWP of 1.  Other gases have GWPs higher or lower than 1, indicating their relative warming effect.

**EF 3.1 GWP Values (100-year time horizon)**

According to the EF 3.1 methodology (as of the latest updates, typically based on IPCC AR6), the following GWP values are used:

*   **CO2 (Carbon Dioxide):** GWP = 1
*   **N2O (Nitrous Oxide):** GWP = 273

**Calculating Climate Impact**

To compare the impact, we need to convert each gas emission into "CO2 equivalents" (CO2e).  This is done by multiplying the mass of the gas by its GWP.

*   **5 kg CO2:**  5 kg * 1 = 5 kg CO2e
*   **1 kg N2O:** 1 kg * 273 = 273 kg CO2e

**Conclusion**

**1 kg of N2O has a significantly greater impact on climate change than 5 kg of CO2.**  Specifically, 1 kg of N2O is equivalent to 273 kg of CO2 in terms of its warming effect, while 5 kg of CO2 is only equivalent to 5 kg of CO2.

**Therefore, N2O has a 54.6 times greater impact on climate change than CO2 in this comparison (273/5 = 54.6).**

**Why N2O is so potent:**

*   **Strong Infrared Absorption:** N2O is very effective at absorbing infrared radiation (heat) emitted from the Earth's surface.
*   **Long Atmospheric Lifetime:** N2O has a long atmospheric lifetime (around 114 years). This means it persists in the atmosphere for a long time, continuing to trap heat.
*   **High Radiative Efficiency:**  It's very efficient at trapping heat per molecule.



**Important Note:**  GWP values can be updated as climate science advances. The values used here are based on the EF 3.1 methodology and the IPCC AR6 assessment. Always refer to the latest official documentation for the most accurate values.
