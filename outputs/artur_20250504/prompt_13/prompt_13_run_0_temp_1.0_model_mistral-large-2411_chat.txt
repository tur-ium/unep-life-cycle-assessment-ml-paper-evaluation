The carbon footprint of producing a high-end consumer laptop can be estimated by considering the emissions from various stages of its lifecycle, including raw material extraction, manufacturing, assembly, transportation, and disposal. While exact figures can vary depending on the specific brand and model, several studies provide useful benchmarks.

One comprehensive study by the Fraunhofer Institute for Reliability and Microintegration (IZM) estimated that the production of a typical laptop emits approximately 249 kg CO2e (Markisch et al., 2010). This estimate includes emissions from the production of components such as the display, motherboard, storage, and battery, as well as the assembly process.

It's important to note that the carbon footprint can vary depending on the specific materials used, the efficiency of the manufacturing process, and the energy sources used for production. High-end laptops might have a higher carbon footprint due to the use of more advanced and energy-intensive components.

Therefore, based on available research, the carbon footprint of the production of a high-end consumer laptop can be estimated at approximately 249 kg CO2e.

Reference:
- Markisch, T., Wotruba, H., Iijima, Y., 2010. Energy Efficient Use of Consumer Electronics and its Impact on Life Cycle Energy Demand.
