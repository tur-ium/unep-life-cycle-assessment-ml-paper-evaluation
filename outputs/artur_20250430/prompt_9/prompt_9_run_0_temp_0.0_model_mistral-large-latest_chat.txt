To systematically compare the two conflicting Life Cycle Assessments (LCAs) of plant-based meat substitutes, follow this analytical framework:

1. **Methodological Comparison**:
   - **Data Sources**: Check if both studies use comparable data sources for emissions, energy use, and agricultural practices (e.g., Ecoinvent, Agri-footprint, or industry-specific data) (Guinée et al., 2002).
   - **System Boundaries**: Compare the scope and boundaries of each study. Ensure they include the same life cycle stages (e.g., raw material extraction, processing, distribution, consumption, and disposal) (ISO, 2006).
   - **Functional Units**: Verify that both studies use equivalent functional units (e.g., per kg of protein, per serving) to ensure a fair comparison (ISO, 2006).
   - **LCIA Methods**: Assess the Life Cycle Impact Assessment (LCIA) methods used. Different methods (e.g., ReCiPe, IMPACT 2002+) can yield varying results (Goedkoop et al., 2009).

2. **Harmonization and Reliability Determination**:
   - **Sensitivity Analysis**: Conduct a sensitivity analysis to understand how variations in key parameters (e.g., emission factors, energy use) affect the outcomes (Heijungs & Suh, 2002).
   - **Uncertainty Analysis**: Evaluate the uncertainty in the data and models used in both studies. Higher uncertainty may indicate less reliable conclusions (Lloyd & Ries, 2007).
   - **Peer Review and Validation**: Check if the studies have been peer-reviewed and validated by independent experts to ensure credibility.
   - **Stakeholder Consultation**: Engage with stakeholders (e.g., industry experts, environmental scientists) to gather insights and validate the findings.

3. **Recommendations for Future LCAs**:
   - **Standardized Protocols**: Adopt standardized LCA protocols and guidelines specific to the food sector to ensure consistency (e.g., PEF - Product Environmental Footprint) (European Commission, 2013).
   - **Transparent Reporting**: Ensure comprehensive and transparent reporting of data sources, methodologies, and assumptions to facilitate comparability and reproducibility.
   - **Collaborative Efforts**: Encourage collaboration among researchers to share data and methodologies, reducing the likelihood of discrepancies.
   - **Regular Updates**: Keep LCA studies updated with the latest data and methodological advancements to reflect current practices and technologies.

By following this framework, discrepancies between LCA studies can be systematically addressed, leading to more reliable and comparable environmental assessments of plant-based meat substitutes.
