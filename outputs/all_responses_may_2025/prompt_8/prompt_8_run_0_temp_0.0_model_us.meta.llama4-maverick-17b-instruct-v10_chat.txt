To conduct a comparative Life Cycle Assessment (LCA) of battery recycling versus battery reuse in energy storage applications, a robust methodological strategy is required to address the challenges posed by data uncertainty, technological variability, and the need for transparent boundary setting and functional unit selection.

First, to handle data uncertainty and technological variability, we will employ a combination of prospective and stochastic LCA approaches. Prospective LCA allows us to model future scenarios based on anticipated advancements in recycling technologies and potential changes in reuse practices (Weidema et al., 2004). Stochastic LCA, through the use of Monte Carlo simulations, will enable us to quantify the uncertainty associated with the lack of direct Life Cycle Inventory (LCI) data for reuse scenarios and the variability in recycling processes (Lloyd & Ries, 2007). For recycling, we will rely on available LCI databases and literature, while for reuse, we will develop scenarios based on expert elicitation and proxy data from similar applications.

Setting boundaries for the LCA involves defining the life cycle stages to be included. For both recycling and reuse, the study will encompass the collection of used batteries, the recycling or refurbishment process, and the subsequent use in energy storage applications, until the final disposal or recycling at the end of the second life. The functional unit will be defined as "the provision of 1 kWh of energy storage capacity over a specified period," ensuring a fair comparison between the two options (Cooper & Fava, 2006). This choice of functional unit is relevant to the energy storage application and allows for the comparison of different battery technologies and reuse or recycling pathways.

To justify methodological assumptions to stakeholders unfamiliar with LCA, we will rely on transparency and sensitivity analysis. All assumptions, including those related to data sources, technological performance, and system boundaries, will be clearly documented. Sensitivity analyses will be conducted to test the robustness of the results to these assumptions, identifying which have the most significant impact on the comparative outcomes (Wei et al., 2015). This approach not only aids in understanding the results but also in communicating the limitations and uncertainties associated with the study.

By adopting a prospective and stochastic LCA approach, carefully defining the system boundaries and functional unit, and maintaining transparency through detailed documentation and sensitivity analysis, this study aims to provide a robust comparison of the environmental impacts of battery recycling versus reuse. This methodological strategy will facilitate informed decision-making for stakeholders and contribute to the ongoing discussion on sustainable management of used batteries in energy storage applications.
