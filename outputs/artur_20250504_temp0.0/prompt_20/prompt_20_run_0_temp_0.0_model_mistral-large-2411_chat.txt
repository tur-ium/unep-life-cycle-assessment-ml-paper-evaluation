In a Life Cycle Assessment (LCA) study for oranges, the storage of carbon in the trees should be considered as a form of temporary carbon sequestration. Here's how to deal with it:

1. **Quantification**: Estimate the amount of carbon stored in the biomass of the orange trees using IPCC factors or local data.

2. **Temporal Aspect**: Consider the time dimension, as carbon is only stored during the life span of the trees. Once the trees are removed, the stored carbon is released back to the atmosphere, either gradually (through decomposition) or abruptly (through burning).

3. **Allocation**: Allocate the temporary carbon storage to the orange production over the lifespan of the trees. This can be done by dividing the total carbon stored by the total yield of oranges over that period.

4. **Reporting**: Clearly report the carbon storage and its temporary nature in your LCA study. It's important to avoid double counting if the carbon dynamics are also considered in other parts of the study, such as land use change.

Dealing with carbon storage in this way ensures that the LCA study accurately reflects the climate impacts of orange production and avoids overestimating the carbon benefits.
