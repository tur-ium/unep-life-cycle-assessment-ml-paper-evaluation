In LCA for oranges, carbon storage in trees should generally be excluded unless there's a net change in carbon stocks over the study period. Mature orchards typically reach a steady state, where carbon sequestered by new growth offsets losses from pruning or tree replacement, resulting in no net storage. Temporary storage (e.g., in young orchards) is often omitted due to methodological complexity and the risk of overestimating climate benefits if eventual release isn’t accounted for. ISO standards prioritize permanent sequestration, and agricultural LCAs usually focus on emissions from inputs (fertilizers, energy) rather than transient biogenic carbon. Exceptions apply if the study explicitly addresses land-use change (e.g., converting grassland to orchard), where initial carbon stock changes must be included.
