A comparative life cycle assessment (LCA) between a specific product and an industry average faces key limitations. **Data granularity and representativeness** are critical: industry averages aggregate data from diverse sources, which may not reflect regional variations, production practices, or material sourcing specific to your product. **System boundary inconsistencies** can skew results, as different studies may exclude stages (e.g., transportation, end-of-life) or use varying allocation methods. **Temporal mismatches** arise if industry data is outdated, failing to account for recent efficiency improvements or regulatory changes. **Methodological choices** (e.g., impact categories, weighting) may differ, complicating direct comparisons. **Uncertainty and transparency** are also concerns, as industry averages often lack detailed metadata, making it hard to validate assumptions. Finally, LCAs typically exclude **socioeconomic factors** (e.g., labor practices, circularity potential), offering an incomplete sustainability picture. To mitigate these issues, pair LCA results with primary data, disclose assumptions, and contextualize findings with complementary metrics.
