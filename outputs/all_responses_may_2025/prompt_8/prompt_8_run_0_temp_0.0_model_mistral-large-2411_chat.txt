Conducting a Life Cycle Assessment (LCA) to compare the environmental impacts of battery recycling versus battery reuse in energy storage applications requires a robust methodological strategy. Here’s an outline of how to approach this:

### Handling Data Uncertainty and Technological Variability

Given the lack of direct Life Cycle Inventory (LCI) data for reuse scenarios and the rapid evolution of recycling technologies, addressing data uncertainty and technological variability is crucial.

1. **Data Collection and Sensitivity Analysis**:
   - **Primary Data**: Collect primary data from pilot projects, industry reports, and academic studies on battery reuse and recycling (e.g., Battery Council International, 2020).
   - **Secondary Data**: Use databases like Ecoinvent for generic LCI data and supplement with industry-specific data (e.g., Ecoinvent, 2021).
   - **Sensitivity Analysis**: Conduct sensitivity analyses to understand the impact of variability in key parameters such as energy consumption, material recovery rates, and transportation distances (e.g., Heijungs and Suh, 2002).

2. **Scenario Analysis**:
   - Develop multiple scenarios to account for technological variability. For example, consider different recycling technologies (e.g., pyrometallurgical vs. hydrometallurgical) and reuse applications (e.g., stationary storage vs. electric vehicle second life) (e.g., Gaines, 2014).

3. **Expert Judgment**:
   - Engage experts in the field to validate assumptions and fill data gaps. This can include academics, industry professionals, and policymakers (e.g., ISO 14044, 2006).

### Setting Boundaries and Choosing an Appropriate Functional Unit

1. **System Boundaries**:
   - Define clear system boundaries that include all relevant life cycle stages: raw material extraction, manufacturing, use phase, end-of-life treatment (recycling or reuse), and transportation (e.g., ISO 14040, 2006).
   - For reuse, consider the additional life cycle stages such as refurbishment and repurposing.

2. **Functional Unit**:
   - Choose a functional unit that allows for a fair comparison. For example, "1 kWh of energy storage capacity provided over the lifetime of the battery" (e.g., Peters et al., 2017).
   - Ensure the functional unit is consistent across both recycling and reuse scenarios to facilitate direct comparison.

### Justifying Methodological Assumptions to Stakeholders

1. **Transparency and Documentation**:
   - Clearly document all assumptions, data sources, and methodological choices. Provide a detailed report that stakeholders can review (e.g., ISO 14044, 2006).

2. **Stakeholder Engagement**:
   - Engage stakeholders early in the process to understand their concerns and expectations. This can include workshops, surveys, and interviews (e.g., Finnveden et al., 2009).

3. **Educational Outreach**:
   - Provide educational materials to explain LCA concepts and methodologies. Use visual aids, such as flowcharts and infographics, to illustrate the life cycle stages and impacts (e.g., Curran, 2012).

4. **Peer Review**:
   - Conduct a peer review of the LCA study to ensure its robustness and credibility. This can involve external experts who can validate the methodological choices and assumptions (e.g., ISO 14044, 2006).

By following this methodological strategy, the LCA will provide a comprehensive and transparent comparison of the environmental impacts of battery recycling versus reuse, addressing data uncertainty and technological variability effectively.
