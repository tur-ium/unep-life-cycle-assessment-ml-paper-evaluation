In a Life Cycle Assessment (LCA) of oranges, the temporary carbon storage in orange trees should be addressed transparently and in accordance with standards such as ISO 14067 and the Product Environmental Footprint (PEF) guidelines. Carbon stored in the woody biomass of trees during their lifetime represents a temporary removal of CO₂ from the atmosphere. However, unless the biomass is harvested and sequestered long-term (e.g., in durable wood products), this carbon is typically released back to the atmosphere after the orchard is cleared and trees decompose or are burned at end of life.

In practice, **LCA practitioners usually report temporary carbon storage as a separate line item and do not subtract it from the total carbon footprint for food products like oranges**, unless it meets long-term storage criteria (typically >100 years). This approach provides transparency and avoids overestimating climate benefits. Following guidelines, I would quantify the annual net carbon uptake of the orange trees and disclose it, but not include it as a permanent offset in the product’s carbon footprint unless the scenario clearly demonstrates long-term sequestration.
