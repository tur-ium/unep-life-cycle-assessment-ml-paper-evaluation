To estimate the carbon footprint of a high-end consumer laptop, we need to consider various stages of its production, including raw material extraction, manufacturing, transportation, and end-of-life disposal or recycling. The carbon footprint is typically measured in tonnes of CO2 equivalent (CO2e), which accounts for the global warming potential of different greenhouse gases.

1. **Raw Material Extraction and Processing**: The production of laptops requires a variety of materials, including metals (like aluminum, copper, and rare earth metals), plastics, and other components. The extraction and processing of these materials have significant environmental impacts. For instance, the mining of metals can lead to deforestation, water pollution, and energy consumption (Mudd, 2007).

2. **Manufacturing**: The manufacturing process, particularly the production of semiconductors and the assembly of laptops, is energy-intensive. A study by Williams et al. (2002) highlighted the energy intensity of semiconductor manufacturing. The global production network for electronics, including laptops, involves complex supply chains that span multiple countries, further complicating the carbon footprint assessment.

3. **Transportation**: Laptops are often manufactured in one region (e.g., Asia) and consumed in another (e.g., North America or Europe), leading to significant transportation-related emissions. The mode of transport (air, sea, or land) affects the carbon footprint, with air transport generally having a higher carbon intensity (Cristea et al., 2013).

4. **End-of-Life**: The disposal or recycling of laptops also impacts their overall carbon footprint. Improper disposal can lead to environmental pollution, while recycling can reduce the demand for primary materials and thus lower the carbon footprint associated with material extraction and processing (Hagelüken, 2006).

To quantify the carbon footprint of a high-end consumer laptop, we can refer to specific studies. A comprehensive life cycle assessment (LCA) study by Andrae and Vaija (2014) estimated the carbon footprint of a laptop. Although the exact figure can vary based on the specific laptop model, manufacturing location, and other factors, we can use this study as a reference.

Andrae and Vaija (2014) estimated that the production of a laptop results in approximately 227 kg CO2e. To convert this into tonnes CO2e, we divide by 1000 (since 1 tonne = 1000 kg), which gives us 0.227 tonnes CO2e.

Therefore, the carbon footprint of producing a high-end consumer laptop is approximately **0.227 tonnes CO2e**.
