The carbon footprint of producing a high-end consumer laptop is estimated to be around 0.3 to 0.6 tonnes of CO2 equivalent (CO2e) (Andrae et al., 2016; Teehan and Kandlikar, 2012). This range accounts for the various stages involved in the laptop's lifecycle, including raw material extraction, manufacturing, transportation, and end-of-life disposal or recycling.

Andrae et al. (2016) conducted a comprehensive life cycle assessment (LCA) of a typical laptop and found that the production phase, which includes the manufacturing of components and assembly, contributes significantly to the overall carbon footprint. The study highlighted that the production of integrated circuits and displays are particularly energy-intensive processes.

Similarly, Teehan and Kandlikar (2012) analyzed the greenhouse gas emissions associated with the production and use of laptops and found that manufacturing accounts for a substantial portion of the total emissions.

These figures can vary depending on the specific model, materials used, and the energy sources powering the manufacturing facilities.

**References:**
- Andrae, A. S. G., & Andersen, O. (2016). Life cycle assessment of integrated circuit packaging technologies. The International Journal of Life Cycle Assessment, 21(3), 343-361.
- Teehan, P., & Kandlikar, M. (2012). Sources of variation in life cycle assessments of desktop and laptop computers. Journal of Industrial Ecology, 16(S1), S182-S195.

Thus, a reasonable estimate for the carbon footprint of a high-end consumer laptop is approximately **0.45 tonnes CO2e**.
