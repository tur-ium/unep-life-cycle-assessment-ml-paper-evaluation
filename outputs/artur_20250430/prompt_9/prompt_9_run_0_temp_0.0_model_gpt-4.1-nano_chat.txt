To systematically compare the two conflicting LCAs of plant-based meat substitutes, I would adopt a structured analytical framework encompassing methodological scrutiny, harmonization steps, and future best practices.

**1. Identify Potential Methodological Differences**

- **Data Sources and Quality:** Examine the origin, recency, and representativeness of input data (e.g., agricultural practices, processing efficiencies). Variations in data quality or regional specificity can significantly influence results (Weidema et al., 2018).

- **System Boundaries:** Assess whether studies include cradle-to-gate, cradle-to-grave, or other boundaries. Differences in scope—such as inclusion/exclusion of land use change, transportation, or packaging—can lead to divergent conclusions (Peters et al., 2017).

- **Functional Units:** Confirm if both studies use the same functional unit (e.g., per kg of product, per serving). Disparities here can distort comparisons, especially if product formulations differ (Heller & Keoleian, 2018).

- **LCIA Methodologies:** Review the impact assessment methods employed (e.g., ReCiPe, TRACI). Different LCIA models emphasize different impact categories, potentially skewing results (Jungbluth et al., 2019).

- **Assumptions and Allocation:** Check assumptions regarding co-products, yield efficiencies, and allocation procedures. Variations can significantly alter environmental footprints (Mekonnen & Hoekstra, 2010).

**2. Steps to Harmonize and Evaluate Reliability**

- **Standardize Data and Boundaries:** Reconcile differences by aligning data sources, updating to recent datasets, and harmonizing system boundaries where possible.

- **Normalize Functional Units:** Convert results to a common functional unit to enable direct comparison.

- **Apply Consistent LCIA Methods:** Recalculate impact scores using a uniform LCIA approach, or at least compare impact category results across methods.

- **Sensitivity and Uncertainty Analysis:** Conduct sensitivity analyses to identify which assumptions or data points most influence outcomes, and evaluate the robustness of each study’s conclusions (Hsu et al., 2014).

- **Critical Appraisal of Transparency and Peer Review:** Assess the transparency of methodologies and peer-review status to gauge reliability.

- **Expert Consultation:** Engage with LCA practitioners or domain experts to interpret nuanced methodological choices.

**3. Recommendations for Future LCAs**

- **Adopt Standardized Protocols:** Use established frameworks like ISO 14044 (ISO, 2006) to ensure consistency.

- **Transparent Reporting:** Clearly document data sources, boundaries, assumptions, and impact assessment choices.

- **Use of Sensitivity and Uncertainty Analyses:** Incorporate these routinely to understand the robustness of findings.

- **Collaborative and Comparative Studies:** Encourage multi-stakeholder, multi-region LCAs to capture variability and improve comparability.

- **Regular Updates:** Keep LCAs current with evolving data, technologies, and methodologies to maintain relevance.

By systematically analyzing methodological differences, harmonizing parameters, and emphasizing transparency, we can better interpret conflicting LCAs and improve their reliability.
