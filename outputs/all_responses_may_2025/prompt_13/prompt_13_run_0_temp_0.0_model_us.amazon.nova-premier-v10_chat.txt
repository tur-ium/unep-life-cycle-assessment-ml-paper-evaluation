The carbon footprint of producing a high-end consumer laptop is estimated to be around 300 to 1,000 kg CO2e. According to a study by Teehan and Kandlikar (2012), the greenhouse gas emissions for a typical laptop range from 240 to 800 kg CO2e over its lifecycle, with a significant portion attributed to the manufacturing phase. Another study by Andrae and Andersen (2010) suggests that the production of a high-end laptop can emit up to 530 kg CO2e, considering the energy-intensive processes involved in fabricating components like microprocessors and memory chips.

These figures encompass the extraction of raw materials, manufacturing of components, assembly, and transportation. High-end laptops, with their advanced processors and larger batteries, tend to be on the higher end of this range due to the increased resource consumption and energy requirements (Williams, 2011).

In summary, the carbon footprint for the production of a high-end consumer laptop is approximately 500-1,000 kg CO2e.
