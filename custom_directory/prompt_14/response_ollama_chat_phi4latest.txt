Determining the exact carbon footprint of producing a high-end consumer laptop involves considering various stages of its lifecycle, including raw material extraction, manufacturing, transportation, and end-of-life disposal or recycling. The carbon footprint can vary significantly based on factors such as the specific model, materials used, energy sources for production, and supply chain logistics.

1. **Raw Material Extraction**: This includes mining metals like aluminum, copper, gold, and rare earth elements, which are essential components of laptops. These processes are energy-intensive and contribute significantly to emissions (Hertwich et al., 2015).

2. **Manufacturing**: The production phase involves assembling the laptop, which requires substantial energy, particularly in semiconductor fabrication for processors and memory chips. Manufacturing is one of the largest contributors to a laptop's carbon footprint (Koomey & Sathaye, 1999).

3. **Transportation**: Moving raw materials and finished products across global supply chains adds to emissions. This includes shipping components from various countries to assembly plants and distributing final products worldwide (Wiedmann et al., 2011).

4. **Use Phase**: While not part of production, the energy consumption during a laptop's use phase also contributes significantly to its overall carbon footprint over its lifetime (Hilty et al., 2006).

5. **End-of-Life**: Disposal or recycling processes can either mitigate or exacerbate emissions depending on how materials are handled.

According to studies and lifecycle assessments, the production of a high-end consumer laptop typically results in approximately 100-250 kg CO2e (kilograms of carbon dioxide equivalent) per unit. This range accounts for variations in design, manufacturing efficiency, and energy sources used during production (Baldé et al., 2015; Koomey & Sathaye, 1999).

To convert this to tonnes CO2e:
- Lower estimate: 100 kg CO2e = 0.1 tonnes CO2e
- Upper estimate: 250 kg CO2e = 0.25 tonnes CO2e

Thus, the carbon footprint of producing a high-end consumer laptop is approximately **0.1 to 0.25 tonnes CO2e**.
