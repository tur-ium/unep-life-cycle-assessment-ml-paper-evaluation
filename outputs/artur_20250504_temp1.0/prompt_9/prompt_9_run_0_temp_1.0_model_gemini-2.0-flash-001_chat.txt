Here's an analytical framework to compare conflicting LCAs of plant-based meat substitutes, aiming to reconcile discrepancies and suggest improvements for future studies:

**1. Methodological Comparison:**

Begin by meticulously examining the studies' methodologies to pinpoint sources of divergence. Key areas include:

*   **Data Sources:** Compare data sources used for inputs (e.g., crop production, transportation, processing, packaging) and outputs (emissions, water use). Differences in geographical location, agricultural practices (e.g., fertilizer type, irrigation method), and industry-specific data will significantly impact results (Poore & Nemecek, 2018). Traceability to original sources and documentation of assumptions are crucial.
*   **System Boundaries:** Assess the scope of each LCA. Did the study include land use change associated with soy production? Are packaging disposal and end-of-life scenarios considered? "Cradle-to-grave" analyses offer a more complete picture, but "cradle-to-gate" studies may focus on specific stages (Roy et al., 2009). Different boundaries can skew results.
*   **Functional Unit:** Ensure both studies use comparable functional units (e.g., 1 kg of product, 100g of protein). Discrepancies here directly influence impact comparisons. Even if seemingly identical, the *definition* of "meat substitute" is important - nutrient profiles and palatability can vary.
*   **Life Cycle Impact Assessment (LCIA) Methods:** Compare the LCIA methods employed (e.g., ReCiPe, CML, TRACI). Different methods emphasize different environmental impacts and may yield contrasting results (Hauschild & Wenzel, 1998). Understanding which impact categories are prioritized in each LCA is essential.

**2. Harmonization and Reliability Assessment:**

*   **Data Normalization:** Attempt to standardize data using common regional or global averages where possible (e.g., energy grids, transportation distances). This helps to isolate the effects of varying assumptions.
*   **Sensitivity Analysis:** Identify key parameters that significantly influence results and assess the impact of varying these parameters within a reasonable range. This highlights the robustness of the conclusions.
*   **Quality Scoring:** Apply established quality scoring frameworks (e.g., ISO 14040/44) to assess data quality, transparency, and methodological rigor of each study. Higher scores indicate greater reliability.
*   **Peer Review:** Consider whether the studies underwent external peer review, which enhances confidence in the findings.

**3. Recommendations for Future LCAs:**

*   **Transparency and Documentation:** Thoroughly document all assumptions, data sources, and methodological choices.
*   **Standardized Methodologies:** Encourage adoption of standardized LCA guidelines and LCIA methods to improve comparability.
*   **Address Uncertainty:** Explicitly address uncertainty through sensitivity analysis and Monte Carlo simulations.
*   **Expanded Scope:** Incorporate broader environmental and social impacts, including biodiversity, land use change, and socioeconomic considerations.
*   **Open Data and Models:** Promote the sharing of LCA data and models to facilitate verification and improvement.

By rigorously comparing methodologies, harmonizing data where feasible, and focusing on transparency, we can better understand conflicting LCA results and improve the robustness of future assessments (Hellweg & Milà i Canals, 2014).
