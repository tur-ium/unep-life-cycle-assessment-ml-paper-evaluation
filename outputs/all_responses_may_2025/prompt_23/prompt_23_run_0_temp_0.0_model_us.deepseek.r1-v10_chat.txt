According to the EF 3.1 impact assessment method, which uses **100-year Global Warming Potential (GWP)** values from the **IPCC Fifth Assessment Report (AR5)**, the climate impact of emissions is calculated as follows:

1. **GWP Values**:
   - **CO₂**: 1 (baseline).
   - **N₂O**: 265 kg CO₂e per kg N₂O.

2. **Calculations**:
   - **5 kg CO₂**: Directly equates to **5 kg CO₂e** (since GWP of CO₂ = 1).
   - **1 kg N₂O**: \( 1 \, \text{kg} \times 265 \, \text{kg CO₂e/kg} = 265 \, \text{kg CO₂e} \).

3. **Comparison**:
   - **1 kg N₂O** has an impact of **265 kg CO₂e**.
   - **5 kg CO₂** has an impact of **5 kg CO₂e**.

**Conclusion**:  
**1 kg of N₂O** has a **significantly greater impact** on climate change than 5 kg of CO₂ under the EF 3.1 method. This is because N₂O has a much higher GWP, making even small quantities far more potent in warming the climate compared to CO₂.
