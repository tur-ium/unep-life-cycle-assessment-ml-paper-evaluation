A Life Cycle Assessment (LCA) comparing our product with an industry average has limitations. Firstly, data variability can skew results; industry averages may not reflect specific practices or technologies used by individual companies. Secondly, regional differences in energy sources, waste management, and raw material extraction impact environmental footprints, making direct comparisons challenging. Thirdly, LCA relies on assumptions and methodological choices, such as system boundaries and allocation methods, which can introduce biases. Fourthly, dynamic market changes mean industry averages may quickly become outdated. Lastly, our product might have unique features or innovations not captured in the average, leading to an incomplete assessment. Therefore, while LCA provides valuable insights, interpreting its results requires caution and context.
