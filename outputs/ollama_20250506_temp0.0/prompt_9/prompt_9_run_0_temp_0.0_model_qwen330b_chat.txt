**Methodological Reasons for Discrepancies**  
Differences in data sources (e.g., regional vs. global databases, primary vs. secondary data), system boundaries (e.g., inclusion of land-use change, supply chain stages), functional units (e.g., per kg vs. per kcal), and life cycle impact assessment (LCIA) methods (e.g., impact categories, characterization factors) can yield conflicting results (ISO 2006; Guinée et al. 2002). For instance, one study might exclude indirect emissions from agricultural inputs, while another includes them, skewing environmental impact estimates. Variations in assumptions about resource efficiency, waste management, or production scalability further compound inconsistencies (Poore & Nemecek 2018).  

**Steps to Harmonize Findings**  
1. **Methodological Audit**: Compare both studies against ISO 14040/14044 standards, mapping differences in data sources, boundaries, functional units, and LCIA methods.  
2. **Sensitivity Analysis**: Test how variations in key assumptions (e.g., water use, land conversion rates) affect outcomes, identifying which factors drive discrepancies.  
3. **Data Harmonization**: Align functional units (e.g., standardize to "per kg product") and use consistent LCIA methods (e.g., IPCC 2019 for global warming potential).  
4. **Uncertainty Quantification**: Assess variability in input data (e.g., agricultural practices, energy mixes) and report confidence intervals to evaluate reliability.  

**Recommendations for Future LCAs**  
- **Standardize Protocols**: Adhere to ISO guidelines and adopt common functional units (e.g., per kg or per serving) and impact categories (e.g., FAO’s Food Systems LCA framework).  
- **Enhance Transparency**: Share primary data, assumptions, and software tools to enable replication and cross-study validation.  
- **Collaborative Benchmarking**: Establish industry-wide databases and peer-review processes to reduce variability in data sources and assumptions (Poore & Nemecek 2018).  
- **Incorporate Dynamic Models**: Account for regional and temporal variability in agricultural systems and climate impacts.  

By addressing these factors, future LCAs can minimize discrepancies and provide more robust, comparable insights for policy and consumer decisions.
