**Methodological Strategy for LCA: Battery Recycling vs. Reuse**

**1. Handling Data Uncertainty and Technological Variability**

Given the lack of direct life cycle inventory (LCI) data for battery reuse and the evolving nature of recycling technologies, I would employ a combination of approaches to address uncertainty and variability:

- **Data Triangulation:** I would use proxy data from similar applications (e.g., second-life batteries in electric vehicles or stationary storage) and supplement with expert elicitation and literature values (e.g., Bobba et al., 2018; Richa et al., 2017). Where possible, I would use scenario analysis to represent a range of plausible technological pathways for both recycling and reuse.
- **Sensitivity Analysis:** Key parameters (e.g., battery lifespan in second use, recycling process yields, energy mix) would be varied systematically to assess their influence on results (Finnveden et al., 2009).
- **Uncertainty Analysis:** Monte Carlo simulation or similar statistical methods would be used to quantify the impact of data uncertainty on the final outcomes (ISO 14044:2006).

**2. System Boundaries and Functional Unit**

- **System Boundaries:** I would adopt a “cradle-to-grave” approach for both scenarios, encompassing raw material extraction, battery manufacturing, use phase (including second-life application for reuse), and end-of-life management (recycling or disposal). For reuse, the boundary would include collection, testing, repurposing, and second-life use, followed by eventual recycling or disposal.
- **Functional Unit:** The functional unit would be defined as “1 kWh of electricity delivered to the grid over the lifetime of the battery system,” ensuring comparability between recycling (which returns materials to the supply chain) and reuse (which extends the service life before recycling). This aligns with recommendations for energy storage LCAs (Ciez & Whitacre, 2016).

**3. Justifying Methodological Assumptions to Stakeholders**

To communicate with stakeholders unfamiliar with LCA:

- **Transparency:** I would clearly document all assumptions, data sources, and methodological choices, explaining the rationale in accessible language (Guinée et al., 2011).
- **Relevance:** I would emphasize that the chosen functional unit reflects the real-world service provided by batteries—energy storage—making the comparison meaningful.
- **Robustness:** By using sensitivity and uncertainty analyses, I would demonstrate that the conclusions are robust across a range of plausible scenarios, not dependent on a single data point or assumption.
- **Iterative Improvement:** I would highlight that the LCA is a living analysis, to be updated as better data and technologies emerge, ensuring ongoing relevance (ISO 14040:2006).
