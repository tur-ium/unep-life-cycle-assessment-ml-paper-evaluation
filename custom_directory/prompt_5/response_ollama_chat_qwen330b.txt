### Understanding the Concepts

**Multi-functionality** refers to situations where a process produces more than one product, or where multiple products are co-produced. In such cases, **allocation** is often required to distribute the environmental impacts among the products.

ISO 15804:2012+A2:2019, like other LCA standards (e.g., ISO 14044), provides a **hierarchy** for handling multi-functionality. The goal is to **minimize the need for allocation** by modifying the system boundary, as allocation can introduce subjectivity and reduce the accuracy of the assessment.

---

### The Hierarchy According to ISO 15804

The standard recommends the following **preference order** for dealing with multi-functionality:

1. **Avoid allocation as far as possible**  
   This is the **preferred option**. It involves **modifying the system boundary**—either by **subdividing** the system or **expanding** it to include all relevant processes and products. This approach ensures that the environmental impacts are directly attributed to the products without the need for arbitrary allocation.

2. **Allocation on the basis of physical relationships**  
   If it is not possible to avoid allocation, the next best option is to use **physical properties** (e.g., mass, energy, volume) to allocate the impacts. This method is more **objective** and **transparent** than economic allocation.

3. **Allocation on the basis of revenue**  
   This is the **least preferred** method. It is based on **economic values**, which can be subjective and may not reflect the actual environmental impact. It is used only when the other two options are not feasible.
