The carbon footprint of producing a high-end consumer laptop is approximately **0.3 to 0.4 tonnes CO₂e**, with a commonly cited midpoint around **0.33 tonnes CO₂e**. This estimate accounts for energy-intensive processes in semiconductor manufacturing, display production, and material extraction (e.g., lithium for batteries). Key studies and sources include:  

- **Apple (2020)**: Reported a production-phase footprint of 0.28 tonnes CO₂e for the 16-inch MacBook Pro, representing 80% of its total lifecycle emissions.  
- **Belkhir & Elmeligi (2018)**: Estimated laptop production emissions at 331 kg CO₂e (0.33 tonnes), emphasizing supply chain complexity and component manufacturing.  
- **Boyd et al. (2009)**: Found manufacturing accounts for 70–80% of a laptop’s total footprint, aligning with later estimates.  

Higher-end models may trend toward the upper end of this range due to advanced components (e.g., GPUs, high-resolution displays).
