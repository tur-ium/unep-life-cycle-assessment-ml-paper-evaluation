To systematically compare the two conflicting Life Cycle Assessment (LCA) studies on plant-based meat substitutes, I propose the following analytical framework:

**Step 1: Identify potential methodological reasons for differences**

1. **Data sources**: Compare the data sources used for the production of plant-based meat substitutes and traditional beef. Are they based on primary data, secondary data, or a combination of both? Are the data sources consistent across both studies?
2. **Boundaries**: Examine the system boundaries of each study. Are they cradle-to-grave, cradle-to-gate, or gate-to-gate? Do they include similar stages of the life cycle (e.g., production, processing, transportation, consumption)?
3. **Functional units**: Verify if the functional units (FU) used in both studies are equivalent. For example, are they expressed per kilogram of product, per serving size, or per nutritional unit? A mismatch in FUs can lead to incomparable results (Rebitzer et al., 2004).
4. **LCIA methods**: Identify the Life Cycle Impact Assessment (LCIA) methods employed in each study. Are they using the same impact categories (e.g., global warming potential, water footprint)? Different LCIA methods can yield different results (Goedkoop et al., 2009).

**Step 2: Harmonize findings or determine which conclusions are more reliable**

1. **Data quality assessment**: Evaluate the quality of the data used in both studies, considering factors like data age, geographical representativeness, and uncertainty (Weidema et al., 2000).
2. **Sensitivity and scenario analysis**: Perform sensitivity and scenario analyses to test the robustness of the results and understand the impact of key assumptions and uncertainties on the conclusions (Saltelli et al., 2000).
3. **Comparison of assumptions**: Compare the assumptions made in both studies regarding production systems, yields, and emissions. Identify areas of divergence and assess their impact on the results.

**Step 3: Recommendations for future LCAs**

1. **Use of primary data**: Encourage the use of primary data, especially for specific production systems and regions.
2. **Transparency and documentation**: Ensure transparency in data sources, assumptions, and methodological choices, and provide detailed documentation to facilitate comparison and replication (ISO, 2006).
3. **Harmonization of LCIA methods**: Promote the use of consistent LCIA methods and impact categories to enable comparability across studies.
4. **Scenario and sensitivity analysis**: Perform thorough scenario and sensitivity analyses to account for uncertainties and provide a more comprehensive understanding of the results.

By following this framework, it is possible to systematically compare the two conflicting LCA studies, identify the sources of discrepancies, and determine which conclusions are more reliable. This will help to provide a more accurate understanding of the environmental advantages of plant-based meat substitutes over traditional beef.
