When conducting a Life Cycle Assessment (LCA) comparing a specific product to an industry average product, several limitations arise. First, industry averages often mask the range of environmental performances within the industry, making direct comparisons less precise. Data quality and availability can differ: your product’s data may be specific and recent, while industry averages may rely on outdated or generalized information, leading to inconsistencies. Assumptions regarding system boundaries, functional units, and impact categories may also differ, potentially biasing results. Additionally, industry averages may not fully capture unique features, innovations, or efficiencies in your product’s design or supply chain, thereby underrepresenting its actual performance. The LCA may also fail to account for regional differences in sourcing, manufacturing, and end-of-life scenarios. Overall, these limitations mean that LCA comparisons to industry averages should be interpreted with caution and contextualized properly.
