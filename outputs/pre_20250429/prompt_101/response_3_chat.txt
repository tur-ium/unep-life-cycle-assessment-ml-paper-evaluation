**Recommendation for LCA Methodology Selection: Solar Electrolysis Hydrogen**

The choice between Attributional (ALCA) and Consequential (CLCA) LCA depends fundamentally on the assessment's goal, particularly whether it aims to describe an existing state or evaluate the consequences of a change (Ekvall & Weidema, 2004).

**1. Pilot-Scale Demonstration Phase:**

*   **Recommended Approach:** Attributional LCA (ALCA).
*   **Goal & Scope:** To quantify the environmental footprint *associated with* the specific pilot plant's operation under defined conditions. Scope should be cradle-to-gate (hydrogen production), focusing on the pilot unit itself and direct inputs.
*   **Data Collection:** Primarily site-specific operational data (energy, water, materials used). Average data can be used for upstream inputs like solar panel manufacturing if supplier specifics are unknown.
*   **Justification:** At pilot scale, the process has negligible market impact. The goal is internal understanding, benchmarking the technology's baseline performance, and identifying operational hotspots. Stakeholders (R&D, internal management) need an accurate account of the *current* process, not hypothetical market shifts (Finnveden et al., 2009). ALCA provides this descriptive snapshot.

**2. Large-Scale Commercial Deployment (Renewable-Dominated Grid):**

*   **Recommended Approach:** Consequential LCA (CLCA).
*   **Goal & Scope:** To evaluate the *system-wide environmental consequences* of introducing large-scale solar electrolysis, considering its effects on the electricity market and displacement of alternative hydrogen sources (e.g., SMR). Scope must include affected markets (electricity, hydrogen).
*   **Data Collection:** Marginal data is crucial.
