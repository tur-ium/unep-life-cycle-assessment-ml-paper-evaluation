When conducting a Life Cycle Assessment (LCA) of oranges, dealing with the storage of carbon in the trees requires careful consideration. The issue at hand is that orange trees, like other perennial crops, act as carbon sinks by absorbing CO2 from the atmosphere through photosynthesis and storing it in their biomass and soil over their lifespan.

To address this in an LCA:

1. **Consider the system boundaries**: Decide whether the study includes the growth phase of the trees. If it does, then the carbon sequestration by the trees should be accounted for.

2. **Choose an appropriate approach**: One common approach is to consider the carbon stored in the trees as a negative emission or a credit during the growth phase. This means that during the cultivation phase, the net emissions associated with orange production would be reduced by the amount of carbon sequestered by the trees.

3. **Use existing data and models**: Utilize existing data on carbon sequestration rates for orange trees and consider dynamic changes in carbon storage over the trees' lifespan.

4. **Consider end-of-life scenarios**: If the study includes the end-of-life phase of the trees (e.g., when they are replaced or harvested), account for the release of stored carbon back into the atmosphere.

By addressing carbon storage in trees in this way, the LCA can provide a more comprehensive and accurate assessment of the environmental impacts associated with orange production.
