To resolve the conflicting conclusions of the LCAs, a systematic comparison focusing on methodological differences is crucial.

**1. Identifying Discrepancies:**

First, I would meticulously compare the studies' methodologies. This involves examining:

*   **Data Sources:** Were the same datasets (e.g., Ecoinvent, Agri-footprint) used? Differences in data quality, regional specificity, and age of datasets can significantly impact results (Sala et al., 2017).
*   **System Boundaries:** Did both studies include the same stages of the life cycle (e.g., feed production, processing, transportation, packaging, waste management)? Omitting key stages can skew results (Finnveden et al., 2009).
*   **Functional Unit:** Was the functional unit (e.g., 1 kg of product, a serving size) identical? Different functional units make direct comparison impossible (ISO 14040, 2006).
*   **LCIA Methods:** Did they use the same impact assessment methods (e.g., ReCiPe, IMPACT 2002+)? Different methods employ varying characterization factors, leading to different impact scores (Hauschild et al., 2018).

**2. Harmonization and Reliability Assessment:**

Next, I would:

*   **Reconcile Data:** If possible, I would attempt to harmonize data by using the same datasets or, where unavailable, performing sensitivity analyses to assess the impact of data variations.
*   **Standardize Boundaries:** Ensure consistent system boundaries across studies. If boundaries differ, I would analyze the sensitivity of the results to the inclusion or exclusion of specific life cycle stages.
*   **Compare Functional Units:** If different, I would convert results to a common functional unit to enable direct comparison.
*   **Analyze LCIA Methods:** Compare the impact categories and characterization factors used. If significant differences exist, I would assess the sensitivity of the results to the choice of LCIA method.
*   **Assess Transparency and Documentation:** Evaluate the studies' transparency regarding data sources, assumptions, and limitations. Well-documented studies are more reliable.

Based on this analysis, I would determine which study's conclusions are more reliable, considering the robustness of the methodology and the sensitivity of the results to methodological choices.

**3. Recommendations for Future LCAs:**

To avoid future discrepancies, future LCAs should:

*   **Adhere to ISO standards:** Strictly follow ISO 14040 and 14044 guidelines for LCA methodology (ISO 14040, 2006; ISO 14044, 2006).
*   **Enhance Transparency:** Provide detailed documentation of data sources, assumptions, and limitations.
*   **Use Consistent Data:** Employ the same, up-to-date, and regionally specific datasets.
*   **Conduct Sensitivity Analyses:** Explore the impact of different methodological choices on the results.
*   **Collaborate and Peer Review:** Encourage collaboration and peer review to ensure methodological rigor and reduce bias.
