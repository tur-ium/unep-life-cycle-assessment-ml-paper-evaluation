To determine which emission has a more significant impact on climate change according to the EF 3.1 (Environmental Footprint 3.1) impact assessment method, we need to compare their Global Warming Potentials (GWPs). The GWP measures how much heat a greenhouse gas traps in the atmosphere over a specific time period, usually 100 years, compared to carbon dioxide (CO₂).

According to the EF 3.1 method, the GWP values are:
- CO₂: 1 (by definition)
- N₂O (Nitrous Oxide): 265

This means that 1 kg of N₂O is equivalent to 265 kg of CO₂ in terms of its impact on global warming over 100 years.

Now, let's calculate the CO₂ equivalents for both emissions:

1. For 5 kg of CO₂:
\[ \text{CO₂ equivalent} = 5 \, \text{kg CO₂} \times 1 = 5 \, \text{kg CO₂e} \]

2. For 1 kg of N₂O:
\[ \text{CO₂ equivalent} = 1 \, \text{kg N₂O} \times 265 = 265 \, \text{kg CO₂e} \]

Comparing the two:
- 5 kg of CO₂ is equivalent to 5 kg CO₂e.
- 1 kg of N₂O is equivalent to 265 kg CO₂e.

Clearly, 1 kg of N₂O has a much greater impact on climate change than 5 kg of CO₂, as it is equivalent to 265 kg of CO₂ in terms of global warming potential.

**Conclusion:** 1 kg of N₂O has a more significant impact on climate change compared to 5 kg of CO₂, based on their respective GWP values.
