In an LCA study for oranges, carbon storage in trees should be accounted for accurately. Trees sequester CO₂, mitigating greenhouse gas impacts. Include the carbon uptake during the tree's growth phase in your system boundary. This involves calculating the biomass of the tree and converting it to carbon content, typically using allometric equations. Subtract this sequestered carbon from the total emissions to reflect the temporary carbon storage. However, if trees are eventually harvested or die and decompose, releasing CO₂, consider this in long-term assessments. Use a dynamic LCA approach if necessary, to account for carbon release over time. This holistic view ensures the study reflects both the benefits of carbon sequestration during the tree's life and the emissions from decomposition, providing a comprehensive environmental impact assessment.
