**Structured Recommendation for Hydrogen Production LCA Methodologies**  

### **1. Pilot-Scale Demonstration Phase**  
**Goal & Scope**: Use **attributional LCA (ALCA)** to quantify direct impacts of the pilot process, focusing on immediate resource use and emissions.  
**Data**: Collect process-specific data (e.g., electrolyzer efficiency, energy inputs) and use average grid mix data for electricity.  
**Justification**: ALCA isolates the pilot’s impacts without modeling market shifts, aligning with stakeholder needs for benchmarking and process optimization (Finnveden et al., 2009). Consequential effects (e.g., grid changes) are negligible at this stage.  

### **2. Large-Scale Deployment in Renewable-Dominated Grid**  
**Goal & Scope**: Apply **consequential LCA (CLCA)** to assess system-wide impacts, including marginal renewable energy expansion and mineral demand for electrolyzers/renewables.  
**Data**: Use marginal data (e.g., new wind/solar installations) and dynamic mineral scarcity indicators (e.g., Cu, Ni).  
**Justification**: CLCA captures indirect effects, such as increased mineral extraction for renewables/electrolyzers and avoided fossil fuels, addressing stakeholder concerns about resource scarcity and long-term decarbonization (Earles & Halog, 2011).  

### **3. Large-Scale Deployment in Fossil-Dominated Grid**  
**Goal & Scope**: Prioritize **CLCA** to model displacement of fossil-based power and potential increases in fossil resource use due to electrolysis demand.  
**Data**: Use marginal grid mix (likely coal/gas) and project fossil fuel depletion impacts via models like OEF (Öko-Institut, 2020).  
**Justification**: CLCA reveals trade-offs between hydrogen’s GHG benefits and increased fossil electricity use, critical for policymakers avoiding carbon lock-in (Zamagni et al., 2012).  

### **Methodological Rationale**  
- **ALCA** suits pilot phases by attributing impacts to the immediate process, aiding transparency for investors (Guinée et al., 2011).  
- **CLCA** is essential for large-scale scenarios to evaluate market-mediated effects (e.g., mineral demand, fossil displacement), aligning with strategic energy planning (Plevin et al., 2014).
