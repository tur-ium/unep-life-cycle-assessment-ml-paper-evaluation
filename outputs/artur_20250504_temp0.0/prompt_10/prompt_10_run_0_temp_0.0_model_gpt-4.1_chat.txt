**Goal and Scope Definition for the Life Cycle Assessment (LCA) Scenario**

---

### **Goal Definition**

**Goal:**  
The goal of this LCA is to compare the environmental impacts of three land use scenarios for a rural community:  
1. Continued conventional agriculture  
2. Conversion of agricultural land to a solar farm  
3. Implementation of agrivoltaics (combined solar and agriculture)

The assessment aims to provide objective, science-based evidence to inform community decision-makers and stakeholders about the potential environmental trade-offs and benefits of each scenario, with a particular focus on greenhouse gas emissions, land use, and other relevant environmental impacts. The results will support consensus-building and guide the community in selecting the most sustainable and beneficial land use option.

**Justification:**  
- The community faces a decision with significant environmental, economic, and social implications.
- Stakeholders have diverse priorities: climate change mitigation, job creation, tax revenue, and agricultural productivity.
- An LCA provides a systematic, transparent, and comparative analysis of the environmental impacts of each scenario, supporting informed decision-making.

---

### **Scope Definition**

**1. Functional Unit:**  
- **1 hectare of land** managed for **1 year** under each scenario.  
  *Justification:* This allows for a direct, fair comparison of environmental impacts per unit area and time, regardless of the land use type.

**2. System Boundaries:**  
- **Cradle-to-grave** approach for each scenario, including:
  - **Agriculture:** Inputs (fertilizers, pesticides, water, energy), farming operations, harvest, and transport to farm gate.
  - **Solar Farm:** Manufacturing, transport, installation, operation, maintenance, and end-of-life management of solar panels and infrastructure.
  - **Agrivoltaics:** Combination of the above, accounting for interactions between solar and agricultural activities.
- **Exclusions:** Downstream processing of agricultural products (beyond farm gate), distribution of electricity (beyond grid connection), unless specifically relevant to local impacts.

**3. Impact Categories:**  
- **Primary:** Greenhouse gas emissions (CO₂, CH₄, N₂O), land use change, and energy use.
- **Secondary (if data allows):** Water use, biodiversity impacts, soil health, and local air quality.

**4. Assumptions and Limitations:**  
- Local data will be used where possible; otherwise, reputable databases and literature will inform inventory data.
- Socio-economic impacts (jobs, tax revenue) are important but will be addressed in a separate, complementary assessment (e.g., social LCA or cost-benefit analysis).

**5. Audience:**  
- Local government, community members, and other stakeholders involved in land use decision-making.

---

### **Summary Table**

| Aspect              | Definition                                                                 |
|---------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Goal**            | Compare environmental impacts of agriculture, solar farm, and agrivoltaics |
| **Functional Unit** | 1 hectare of land managed for 1 year                                       |
| **System Boundary** | Cradle-to-grave for each scenario                                          |
| **Impact Categories**| GHG emissions, land use, energy use, (optionally: water, biodiversity)    |
| **Audience**        | Community decision-makers and stakeholders                                 |

---

### **Justification of Choices**

- **Functional unit** ensures comparability across scenarios.
- **System boundaries** capture all relevant life cycle stages for each land use.
- **Impact categories** reflect the main environmental concerns raised (GHG, land use) and allow for broader sustainability considerations.
- **Goal and audience** are tailored to the community’s needs for evidence-based, transparent decision-making.

**In summary:**  
The LCA’s goal and scope are designed to provide a fair, comprehensive, and relevant comparison of the environmental impacts of the three land use options, supporting the community’s decision-making process with robust scientific evidence.
