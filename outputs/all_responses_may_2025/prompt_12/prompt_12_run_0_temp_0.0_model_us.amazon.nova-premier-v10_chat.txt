ReCiPe 2016 and CML-IA are both life cycle impact assessment (LCIA) methods primarily designed for evaluating environmental impacts rather than social impacts. ReCiPe 2016, developed by Mark Huijbregts and colleagues, provides a harmonized set of characterization factors at the midpoint and endpoint levels for a wide range of environmental impact categories, such as climate change, ozone depletion, and human toxicity.

CML-IA, developed by the Centrum voor Milieukunde (Institute of Environmental Sciences) at Leiden University, is another widely used method that focuses on environmental impacts and offers midpoint indicators for various impact categories.

For social life cycle assessment (S-LCA), these methods are not directly applicable because they do not cover social and socio-economic aspects. Instead, you should consider using methods specifically tailored for assessing social impacts throughout the life cycle of a product or service. Some of the commonly used S-LCA methods include:

1. **UNEP/SETAC Guidelines for Social Life Cycle Assessment of Products (2009)**: This guideline provides a comprehensive framework for assessing social impacts and stakeholders' issues in the life cycle of products.

2. **Social Hotspots Database (SHDB)**: This tool and method allow for the identification and assessment of social risks associated with different economic sectors and regions.

3. **Product Social Impact Life Cycle Assessment (PSILCA)**: This method focuses on the social impacts of products and includes various stakeholder categories and impact subcategories.

4. **SOLCA (Social Life Cycle Assessment)**: This method aims to integrate social aspects into the life cycle assessment framework, focusing on workers, consumers, local communities, and society.

When conducting a social life cycle assessment, it is crucial to use methods and tools specifically designed for evaluating social impacts to ensure that the assessment is relevant and comprehensive.
