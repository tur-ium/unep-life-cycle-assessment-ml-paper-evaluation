To guide the energy company in selecting the appropriate goal, scope, and data collection methodology for assessing the life cycle impacts of their novel hydrogen production process, the following recommendations are provided for each scenario:

### Pilot-scale Demonstration Phase
**Goal and Scope:**
- **Goal:** To understand the direct environmental impacts of the pilot-scale hydrogen production process.
- **Scope:** Focus on the immediate impacts of the production process, including material and energy inputs, emissions, and waste outputs.

**Methodological Choice:**
- **Attributional Life Cycle Assessment (ALCA):** This approach is suitable for the pilot phase as it provides a snapshot of the current environmental impacts without considering future market changes. ALCA is straightforward and less data-intensive, making it ideal for early-stage assessments (Guinée et al., 2002).

**Data Collection:**
- Collect detailed data on material and energy inputs, emissions, and waste outputs from the pilot plant.
- Use industry-standard databases for background processes (e.g., Ecoinvent).

### Large-scale Commercial Deployment in an Electricity Grid Dominated by Renewable Energy
**Goal and Scope:**
- **Goal:** To evaluate the broader environmental impacts of large-scale hydrogen production in a renewable-dominated grid, including indirect effects on the energy market.
- **Scope:** Include the entire life cycle of hydrogen production, from raw material extraction to end-of-life disposal, considering market-mediated effects.

**Methodological Choice:**
- **Consequential Life Cycle Assessment (CLCA):** This approach is necessary to capture the system-wide impacts of large-scale deployment, including changes in the energy market and resource use (Ekvall & Weidema, 2004).

**Data Collection:**
- Gather data on the hydrogen production process, including energy and material inputs.
- Collect market data on the renewable energy grid, including supply and demand dynamics.
- Use economic models to predict market responses to large-scale hydrogen production.

### Large-scale Deployment in a Grid Dominated by Fossil-based Electricity
**Goal and Scope:**
- **Goal:** To assess the environmental impacts of hydrogen production in a fossil-dominated grid, focusing on fossil resource use, mineral resource scarcity, and global warming.
- **Scope:** Include the entire life cycle, considering the impacts of fossil fuel use and potential market shifts.

**Methodological Choice:**
- **Consequential Life Cycle Assessment (CLCA):** This approach is essential to understand the broader implications of hydrogen production in a fossil-dominated grid, including potential shifts in fossil fuel use and emissions (Weidema, 2003).

**Data Collection:**
- Collect data on the hydrogen production process and fossil fuel inputs.
- Gather market data on the fossil-based electricity grid, including supply and demand dynamics.
- Use economic models to predict market responses and potential shifts in fossil fuel use.

### Justification for Methodological Choices
**Attributional LCA:**
- Suitable for the pilot phase as it provides a clear and direct assessment of the current environmental impacts without the complexity of market-mediated effects.
- Ideal for early-stage decision-making and process optimization (Guinée et al., 2002).

**Consequential LCA:**
- Necessary for large-scale deployment scenarios to capture the broader system-wide impacts, including market responses and indirect effects.
- Essential for understanding the long-term environmental consequences and informing strategic decisions (Ekvall & Weidema, 2004; Weidema, 2003).

By selecting the appropriate LCA approach for each scenario, the energy company can gain a comprehensive understanding of the environmental impacts of their hydrogen production process, ensuring informed decision-making and stakeholder satisfaction.
