Here's a structured recommendation for the energy company, focusing on goal, scope, and data collection methodologies across the three scenarios:

**Overall Approach:** A hybrid approach, combining attributional and consequential LCA, is recommended to provide a comprehensive understanding of the hydrogen production process's impacts.

**Scenario 1: Pilot-Scale Demonstration Phase**

*   **Goal & Scope:** Primarily **attributional LCA** focusing on the pilot plant's boundaries. The goal is to quantify direct resource use and emissions during the pilot. The scope should include well-to-gate analysis, encompassing all inputs and outputs of the pilot plant, excluding indirect impacts.
*   **Data Collection:** Collect primary data meticulously from the pilot plant itself (e.g., energy consumption, water usage, materials input, waste generation). Use background databases for upstream material and energy flows.
*   **Justification:** Attributional LCA provides a transparent, detailed inventory of the pilot plant's environmental footprint, answering the immediate question of resource use and emissions at this scale (Finnveden et al., 2009). This information is crucial for identifying areas for improvement before scaling up.
*   **Answering the question:** Attributional LCA provides a first estimate of resource use, scarcity and global warming potential.

**Scenario 2: Large-Scale Deployment in Renewable-Dominated Grid**

*   **Goal & Scope:** A **consequential LCA** with an expanded system boundary to include the impact on electricity market and renewable infrastructure. The goal is to assess how hydrogen production affects the overall electricity grid, emphasizing changes in resource use, mineral scarcity, and global warming potential due to the hydrogen production demand.
*   **Data Collection:** Focus on marginal electricity sources displaced or brought online due to hydrogen production. Model market interactions (e.g., capacity expansion of renewable plants). Incorporate projections of future grid mix scenarios.
*   **Justification:** In a renewable-dominated grid, the impact of hydrogen production is less about direct emissions and more about influencing electricity market dynamics. Consequential LCA identifies the marginal changes in resource use and emissions induced by the novel technology (Ekvall & Weidema, 2004). Stakeholders are interested in long-term environmental implications.
*   **Answering the question:** Consequential LCA provides information on the indirect effects of the hydrogen production in the electrical grid.

**Scenario 3: Large-Scale Deployment in Fossil-Based Grid**

*   **Goal & Scope:** A **consequential LCA**, but with a focus on fossil fuel extraction, processing, and combustion. The goal is to assess the impact on fossil resource depletion, emissions, and mineral scarcity. The system boundary includes the direct impact of the hydrogen plant itself and the upstream implications of fossil-based electricity.
*   **Data Collection:** Analyze marginal electricity sources, which are likely fossil-fuel based. Assess potential impacts on fossil resource extraction and processing (e.g., coal mining, natural gas extraction).
*   **Justification:** Because the marginal electricity will likely be produced by fossil fuel power plants, the consequential LCA will focus on the fossil fuel value chain. Stakeholders are interested in understanding how the hydrogen production process affects dependence on fossil fuels and increases greenhouse gas emissions.
*   **Answering the question:** Consequential LCA can provide important insights into the effects of fossil fuels on mineral resource scarcity, as well as the overall global warming potential of the hydrogen production method.

**Hybrid Approach:** Conduct an attributional analysis for the hydrogen production plant in parallel. Compare the direct impact to the total impact shown in the consequential LCA.

*   Finnveden, G., Hauschild, M. Z., Ekvall, T., Guinée, J., Heijungs, R., Hellweg, S., ... & Rebitzer, G. (2009). Recent developments in life cycle assessment. *Journal of Environmental Management*, *91*(1), 1-21.
