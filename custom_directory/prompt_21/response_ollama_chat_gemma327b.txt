## LCA Limitations: Our Product vs. Industry Average

Our Life Cycle Assessment (LCA) comparing our product to the industry average provides valuable insights, but has limitations. Primarily, defining a true "industry average" is challenging. Data availability varies greatly, forcing us to rely on aggregated, potentially outdated, or incomplete sources. This introduces uncertainty into the baseline comparison.

Furthermore, industry averages often represent a 'lowest common denominator', masking innovation. Our product may outperform in specific impact categories *not* fully captured in the average, or utilize newer, more sustainable materials not yet widely adopted. 

The LCA scope also focuses on core functionality.  Features differentiating our product (e.g., durability, repairability) impacting long-term environmental benefits aren't fully quantified in this comparison. 

Finally, geographical variations in manufacturing & end-of-life treatment aren't fully accounted for in the industry average data. Therefore, while the LCA highlights potential advantages, it's crucial to view the results as indicative rather than absolute, and consider the broader context of product performance and innovation.
