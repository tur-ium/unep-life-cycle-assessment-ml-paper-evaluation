The **ReCiPe 2016** and **CML-IA** life cycle impact assessment (LCIA) methods are **primarily designed for environmental life cycle assessment (LCA)** and **do not include social impact categories**. Here's a detailed breakdown of their applicability to **social life cycle assessment (S-LCA)**:

---

### **1. ReCiPe 2016 (Huijbregts, 2016)**
- **Purpose**: ReCiPe is an environmental LCIA method that quantifies environmental impacts (e.g., climate change, water use, toxicity) using a multi-criteria approach. It includes **12 impact categories** (e.g., global warming, eutrophication, human toxicity).
- **Social Relevance**: **No social impact categories** are included. It focuses solely on environmental endpoints.
- **Conclusion**: **Not suitable** for S-LCA. It cannot assess social issues like labor rights, health, or community impacts.

---

### **2. CML-IA (Centrum voor Milieukunde)**
- **Purpose**: CML-IA is another environmental LCIA method developed by the Netherlands' Centrum voor Milieukunde. It includes **10-12 impact categories** (e.g., climate change, ozone depletion, photochemical smog).
- **Social Relevance**: **No social impact categories** are included. Like ReCiPe, it is strictly environmental.
- **Conclusion**: **Not suitable** for S-LCA. It lacks frameworks for social indicators.

---

### **Why These Methods Are Not Suitable for S-LCA**
- **Scope Mismatch**: S-LCA focuses on **social impacts** (e.g., labor rights, health, safety, community well-being), which are **qualitative and context-dependent**, unlike the **quantitative, environmental metrics** these methods address.
- **Lack of Social Indicators**: Neither method includes social impact categories (e.g., child labor, forced labor, access to clean water) or frameworks for social data collection.
- **Methodological Differences**: S-LCA often relies on **stakeholder engagement** and **qualitative data**, whereas environmental LCIA methods are based on **quantitative, science-based models**.

---

### **Alternative Approaches for Social LCA**
If you need to assess social impacts, consider the following dedicated S-LCA frameworks:
1. **ISO 21930:2017 (Sustainability in building and construction)**: Includes social aspects but is not a standalone S-LCA method.
2. **Social Hotspots Database (SHD)**: A tool for identifying social risks in supply chains.
3. **S-LCA Framework by the International Labour Organization (ILO)**: Provides guidelines for assessing social impacts.
4. **Socio-economic LCA (SE-LCA)**: Integrates social and environmental impacts, often using **multi-criteria analysis**.
5. **Social Life Cycle Assessment (S-LCA) by the European Commission**: A structured approach for evaluating social impacts.

---

### **Can They Be Adapted?**
While ReCiPe and CML-IA are not designed for S-LCA, you could **combine them with social LCA methods** in a **hybrid approach**:
- Use ReCiPe/CML-IA for environmental impacts.
- Use a separate S-LCA method (e.g., SHD, ILO framework) for social impacts.
- Integrate results using **multi-criteria decision analysis (MCDA)** or **stakeholder prioritization**.

However, this requires careful alignment of data and interpretation, as the two domains (environmental vs. social) have different metrics and goals.

---

### **Key Takeaway**
- **ReCiPe 2016 and CML-IA are not suitable for social LCA** as they lack social impact categories.
- For S-LCA, use **dedicated frameworks** like the ILO S-LCA, SHD, or ISO 21930, which are designed to address social issues comprehensively.
