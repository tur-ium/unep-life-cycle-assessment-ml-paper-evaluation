In a slaughterhouse setting, the multi-output allocation method that is often recommended is the **physical units method**. This approach allocates joint costs based on measurable physical quantities such as weight or volume of different outputs (e.g., meat cuts, hides, and by-products). 

The rationale for using this method in a slaughter process includes:

1. **Direct Correlation**: The physical units method directly correlates the allocation with tangible outputs, making it straightforward to apply since each product's share of joint costs is proportional to its measurable quantity.

2. **Simplicity and Transparency**: This method is simple to implement and understand, providing transparency in cost allocation which can be crucial for both internal management and external reporting purposes.

3. **Reflects Resource Usage**: It reflects the actual resource usage more accurately than other methods like sales value or net realizable value, as it considers the physical effort and resources consumed during processing.

4. **Consistency with Industry Practices**: The method aligns well with industry practices where outputs are often valued based on their weight or volume, facilitating comparisons across different operations.

While this method is effective in many scenarios, it's important to consider any significant differences in value among products that might not be captured by physical measures alone. In such cases, supplementary methods like sales value at split-off could be used for a more comprehensive allocation strategy.
