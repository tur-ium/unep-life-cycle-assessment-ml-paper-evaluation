ReCiPe 2016 and CML-IA are **environmental life cycle impact assessment (LCIA) methods** designed to evaluate environmental impacts (e.g., climate change, resource depletion, toxicity). They are **not suitable for social life cycle assessment (S-LCA)**, which focuses on **social and socio-economic impacts** (e.g., labor rights, human health, community well-being, equity). Here’s why:

---

### Key Differences Between Environmental LCIA and S-LCA:
1. **Scope and Indicators**:
   - **Environmental LCIA** (ReCiPe/CML-IA): Quantifies impacts like CO₂ emissions, water use, or ecotoxicity.
   - **S-LCA**: Assesses social risks and benefits, such as worker safety, fair wages, child labor, or community displacement.

2. **Methodological Frameworks**:
   - S-LCA requires **dedicated frameworks** like the **UNEP/SETAC Guidelines** or the **Social Hotspots Database (SHDB)**, which include indicators tied to stakeholder categories (workers, local communities, consumers, etc.).

3. **Data Sources**:
   - Environmental LCIA relies on **biophysical data** (e.g., emissions, resource flows).
   - S-LCA uses **socio-economic data** (e.g., labor practices, human rights records, governance metrics).

---

### Tools and Methods for S-LCA:
- **UNEP/SETAC S-LCA Guidelines**: The standard framework for structuring S-LCA studies.
- **PSILCA**: A database providing social impact data across supply chains.
- **SHDB (Social Hotspots Database)**: Identifies social risks in global supply chains.
- **S-LCA-specific impact categories**: E.g., "forced labor risk," "gender equity," or "access to education."

---

### Can Environmental and Social Assessments Be Combined?
While environmental and social assessments are distinct, they can be **complementary** in holistic sustainability analyses. For example:
- Use **ReCiPe/CML-IA** for environmental impacts.
- Pair with **S-LCA methods** (e.g., PSILCA or SHDB) for social impacts.
- Tools like **SimaPro** or **openLCA** allow integration of both approaches in parallel.

---

### Conclusion:
ReCiPe 2016 and CML-IA are **not appropriate for S-LCA**. For social impacts, adopt dedicated S-LCA frameworks and databases. Combining both environmental and social methods provides a more complete sustainability assessment.
