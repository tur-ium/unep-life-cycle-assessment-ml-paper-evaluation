The carbon footprint of a high-end consumer laptop can be estimated by considering the emissions from various stages of its life cycle, including raw material extraction, manufacturing, transportation, use, and end-of-life disposal. However, the majority of a laptop's carbon footprint comes from its production and use phases.

A study by Apple Inc. (2021) reported that the production of a 13-inch MacBook Pro emits approximately 173 kg CO2e. Another study by Dell (2020) estimated that the production of a high-end laptop emits around 349 kg CO2e. These values vary due to differences in methodologies, data sources, and product specifications.

For a more general estimate, we can refer to a study by the Fraunhofer Institute (2018), which calculated the production of a laptop weighing 2 kg with a 14-inch display emits around 280 kg CO2e. This value includes emissions from raw material extraction, manufacturing, and transportation.

To provide a single number for a high-end consumer laptop, we can take an average of the above estimates:

(173 kg CO2e + 349 kg CO2e + 280 kg CO2e) / 3 = 267.33 kg CO2e

Converting this to tonnes CO2e:

267.33 kg CO2e = 0.26733 tonnes CO2e

So, the carbon footprint of the production of a high-end consumer laptop is approximately **0.267 tonnes CO2e**.

- Dell (2020). Corporate Social Responsibility Report: Product Carbon Footprint.
- Fraunhofer Institute (2018). Carbon Footprint of Laptops: A Life Cycle Assessment.
