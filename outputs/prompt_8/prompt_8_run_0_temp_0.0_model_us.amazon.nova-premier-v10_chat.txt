To conduct a Life Cycle Assessment (LCA) comparing the environmental impacts of battery recycling versus battery reuse in energy storage applications, a robust methodological strategy must be employed, especially given the absence of direct Life Cycle Inventory (LCI) data for reuse scenarios and the rapid evolution of recycling technologies.

**Handling Data Uncertainty and Technological Variability**

Given the data gaps and technological dynamism, a combination of primary and secondary data sources will be utilized. For recycling, recent industry reports and academic studies (e.g., Harper et al., 2019) will provide baseline LCI data. For reuse scenarios, proxy data from similar applications and expert consultations will be essential. Sensitivity and uncertainty analyses will be conducted to address data variability. Monte Carlo simulations can quantify the impact of uncertain parameters on the overall results (Heijungs and Suh, 2002). Additionally, scenario analysis will explore different technological pathways and their potential future impacts, reflecting the rapid evolution in recycling technologies (Dunn et al., 2012).

**Setting Boundaries and Choosing an Appropriate Functional Unit**

The system boundaries will encompass cradle-to-grave stages for both recycling and reuse pathways, including raw material extraction, manufacturing, transportation, use phase, and end-of-life processes. The functional unit will be defined as "the provision of 1 kWh of energy storage capacity over a specified lifecycle," ensuring comparability between recycling and reuse scenarios. This functional unit accounts for the performance and longevity differences between reused and recycled batteries. The use phase will consider degradation rates and capacity fade, which are critical for energy storage applications (Notter et al., 2010).

**Justifying Methodological Assumptions to Stakeholders**

To justify methodological assumptions to stakeholders unfamiliar with LCA, clear and transparent communication is key. The rationale behind the choice of data sources, including the use of proxy data for reuse scenarios, will be explained with references to established LCA guidelines (ISO 14040, 2006). The importance of sensitivity and uncertainty analyses in understanding the robustness of the results will be highlighted, demonstrating how these methods account for data gaps and variability. Visual aids, such as flowcharts and graphs, will be used to illustrate system boundaries and the impacts of different life cycle stages. Stakeholder workshops and feedback sessions will be organized to ensure their concerns and perspectives are incorporated into the study, enhancing credibility and acceptance of the findings (Guinée et al., 2011).

In conclusion, this LCA will provide a comprehensive comparison of battery recycling and reuse by addressing data uncertainties, setting appropriate boundaries, and clearly communicating methodological choices. This approach ensures that the results are reliable, transparent, and understandable to all stakeholders involved.
