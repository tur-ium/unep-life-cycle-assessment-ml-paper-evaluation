Here's why:

Life Cycle Assessment (LCA), as defined by ISO 14044, is specifically designed to assess the environmental impacts associated with *all* stages of a product's life – from raw material extraction ("cradle") through manufacturing, use, and end-of-life ("grave"). 

Option C directly addresses this by focusing on a change in *how* a material is made (new synthesis route) and its impact on the environmental footprint (carbon footprint) of a final *product* (plastic sheet).  It allows for a cradle-to-grave analysis of the impact of that change.

Let's look at why the others aren't ideal:

*   **A:** Focuses on *policies* and socio-economic factors, not environmental impacts across a product's life.
*   **B:** Primarily concerns *human health* and *clinical outcomes*, which are outside the scope of standard LCA (though can be *included* as impact categories).
*   **D:** Deals with a biological life cycle, not a product's life cycle.
