# Goal and Scope Definition for Solar Farm vs. Agricultural Land LCA

## Appropriate Goal Definition

The goal of this life cycle assessment (LCA) should be:

**"To compare the environmental impacts, economic benefits, and social implications of three land-use scenarios for the rural community: (1) maintaining current agricultural practices, (2) converting to a dedicated solar farm, and (3) implementing an agrivoltaic system that combines solar power generation with agricultural production."**

### Justification for Goal:

1. **Comprehensive approach**: The goal addresses the multiple dimensions (environmental, economic, social) that stakeholders have expressed interest in, rather than focusing solely on greenhouse gas emissions.

2. **Comparative framework**: By explicitly comparing three alternatives, the LCA will provide decision-makers with clear trade-offs between options.

3. **Stakeholder inclusivity**: The goal acknowledges the diverse interests mentioned in the scenario (climate change mitigation, job creation, tax revenue, and land productivity).

## Appropriate Scope Definition

### Functional Unit:
"The environmental and socioeconomic impacts per hectare of land over a 30-year period."

### System Boundaries:
- **Temporal boundary**: 30 years (typical solar farm lifetime)
- **Spatial boundary**: The specific land area under consideration in the rural community
- **Life cycle stages**: 
  * For agriculture: Inputs (seeds, fertilizers, pesticides, machinery), cultivation, harvesting, processing, and transportation
  * For solar: Manufacturing of panels, installation, operation, maintenance, and end-of-life disposal/recycling
  * For agrivoltaics: Combined elements of both systems with consideration of synergies and trade-offs

### Impact Categories:
1. **Environmental**:
   - Global warming potential (CO₂e)
   - Land use change impacts
   - Water consumption and quality
   - Biodiversity impacts
   - Soil quality

2. **Economic**:
   - Job creation (number and quality)
   - Tax revenue generation
   - Long-term economic resilience
   - Energy cost savings

3. **Social**:
   - Food security implications
   - Community acceptance
   - Visual/aesthetic impacts
   - Skills development opportunities

### Data Requirements:
- Agricultural yields and inputs for current and projected practices
- Solar panel efficiency, lifespan, and maintenance requirements
- Agrivoltaic system design specifications and expected synergies
- Local economic multipliers for different industries
- Job creation potential across scenarios

### Assumptions and Limitations:
- Specification of which agricultural practices are being assessed
- Assumptions about solar technology evolution over the 30-year period
- Consideration of local climate conditions affecting both agriculture and solar production
- Acknowledgment of uncertainty in long-term economic projections

## Justification for Scope:

1. The 30-year timeframe aligns with typical solar installation lifespans while allowing for meaningful agricultural comparisons.

2. The per-hectare functional unit enables scaling to different project sizes and facilitates direct comparison between options.

3. The comprehensive impact categories address all stakeholder concerns mentioned in the scenario.

4. Including both environmental and socioeconomic factors provides a holistic assessment that can better inform community consensus-building.

5. The agrivoltaic option is properly scoped as a distinct alternative rather than merely a compromise, acknowledging its unique characteristics and potential synergies.

This goal and scope definition would provide the community with a balanced, evidence-based assessment to support their decision-making process while addressing the diverse priorities of different stakeholders.
