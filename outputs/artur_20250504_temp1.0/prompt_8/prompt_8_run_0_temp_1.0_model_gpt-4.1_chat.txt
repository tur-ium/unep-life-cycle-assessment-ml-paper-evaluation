**Methodological Strategy for Comparing Battery Recycling and Reuse via LCA**

**1. Handling Data Uncertainty and Technological Variability**

Given the lack of direct Life Cycle Inventory (LCI) data for battery reuse and the rapid advancement in recycling technologies, I will adopt a scenario-based, hybrid modeling approach (Finnveden et al. 2009). For recycling, I will consult recent literature and industry reports to gather the most up-to-date data, documenting the range in process efficiencies, yields, and energy mixes (Dunn et al. 2015). For battery reuse (e.g., in stationary energy storage), I will apply process- and input-based modeling, leveraging analogs from secondary use studies (Bobba et al. 2018) and combining them with primary data from industry stakeholders where available. 

To address uncertainty:

- I will employ Monte Carlo simulations to capture parameter uncertainty, representing ranges for key variables such as lifespan, transport energy, and electricity mix (Igos et al. 2019).
- Technological variability will be modeled through sensitivity analyses, exploring future scenarios for both recycling (e.g., hydrometallurgical vs. pyrometallurgical processes) and reuse (e.g., different repurposing methods, system designs).
- Data quality indicators (temporal, geographical, and technological representativeness) will be documented using the pedigree matrix approach (Weidema & Wesnæs 1996).

**2. System Boundaries and Functional Unit Selection**

System boundaries will be defined as **cradle-to-grave**, encompassing raw material acquisition, manufacturing, initial use (EV application), collection, transportation, and subsequent end-of-life options:

- For **reuse**, boundaries will include decommissioning, repurposing/refurbishment, second life in stationary storage, and eventual recycling/disposal (e.g., Bobba et al. 2018).
- For **recycling**, boundaries include collection, sorting, recycling processes, and materials recovery.

The functional unit will be **one kWh of electricity delivered to the grid over the entire second-life or recycled material workflow**. This unit ensures comparability by reflecting the core service provided by the battery in both pathways (ISO 14040:2006; Siret et al. 2022). This choice also allows linking impact results to grid-scale decarbonization goals.

**3. Justifying Methodological Assumptions to Non-LCA Stakeholders**

To ensure transparency and credibility for stakeholders unfamiliar with LCA, I will:

- Clearly communicate the rationale behind chosen system boundaries and functional unit, linking both directly to the service the batteries are expected to deliver (ISO 14044:2006).
- Explain the reliance on sensitivity and scenario analyses by drawing parallels to probabilistic risk assessment in business and engineering.
- Use clear visualizations (e.g., tornado diagrams) to demonstrate where uncertainty most significantly affects results.
- Relate assumptions (e.g., battery second-life duration, recycling efficiencies) to real-world variability and ongoing technological change, stressing that results are indicative rather than prescriptive, and offering scenario ranges instead of point estimates (Pizzol et al. 2017).
- Provide a concise summary of critical assumptions and data sources in all reporting.
