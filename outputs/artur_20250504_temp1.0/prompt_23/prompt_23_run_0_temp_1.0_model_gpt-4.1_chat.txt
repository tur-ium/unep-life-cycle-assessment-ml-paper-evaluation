Let's analyze the climate change impact of emissions according to the **EF 3.1 impact assessment method** by the European Commission, focusing on **5 kg of CO₂** vs **1 kg of N₂O**.

---

## 1. **Global Warming Potentials (GWP) in EF 3.1**

The **EF (Environmental Footprint) 3.1 method** adheres to the IPCC (Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change) characterization factors to assess climate change potential. The EF 3.1 method uses **GWP100** factors (100-year time horizon):

- **CO₂ (carbon dioxide):** GWP100 = **1**
- **CH₄ (methane):** GWP100 = **28** (or 25 in older methods)
- **N₂O (nitrous oxide):** GWP100 = **265**

> These values are based on the IPCC AR5 report and currently adopted in EF 3.1.

---

## 2. **Calculation of Impact (in kg CO₂-eq)**

To compare the climate change impact, convert both emissions to **kg CO₂-equivalents (CO₂-eq)** using GWP100 factors:

### **For 5 kg CO₂:**

\[
\text{Impact} = 5\,\text{kg} \times 1 = \mathbf{5\,kg\,CO_2\text{-}eq}
\]

### **For 1 kg N₂O:**

\[
\text{Impact} = 1\,\text{kg} \times 265 = \mathbf{265\,kg\,CO_2\text{-}eq}
\]

---

## 3. **Conclusion**

**1 kg of N₂O** has far **more impact** on climate change than **5 kg of CO₂**.

- **1 kg N₂O = 265 kg CO₂-eq**
- **5 kg CO₂ = 5 kg CO₂-eq**

So, **1 kg N₂O is 53 times more impactful than 5 kg CO₂.**

---

## 4. **Why?**

- **N₂O** has a much **higher global warming potential** due to its higher infrared absorption and much longer atmospheric lifetime than CO₂.
- According to the EF 3.1 method, these differences are captured in the GWP factors.

---

### **Summary Table**

| Emission      | Amount (kg) | GWP100 | Impact (kg CO₂-eq) |
|---------------|-------------|--------|--------------------|
| CO₂           | 5           | 1      | 5                  |
| N₂O           | 1           | 265    | 265                |

### **Final Answer**

> **According to the EF 3.1 method, 1 kg of N₂O has much more impact on climate change than 5 kg of CO₂ (265 kg CO₂-eq vs 5 kg CO₂-eq) because the global warming potential (GWP) of N₂O is 265 times higher than that of CO₂.**
