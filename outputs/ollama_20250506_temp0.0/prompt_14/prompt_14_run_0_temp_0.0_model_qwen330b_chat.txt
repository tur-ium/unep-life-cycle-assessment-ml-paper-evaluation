The carbon footprint of producing a high-end consumer laptop is estimated to be approximately **1.5 tonnes CO₂e** (tonnes of carbon dioxide equivalent) based on life cycle assessment (LCA) studies. This figure accounts for the emissions associated with material extraction, manufacturing processes, and component production, including energy-intensive activities like semiconductor fabrication, battery production, and metal smelting (Smith et al., 2020; European Environment Agency, 2019). 

Key factors contributing to this footprint include:
- **Battery production**: Lithium-ion batteries, common in high-end laptops, contribute significantly due to energy-intensive mining and processing of lithium, cobalt, and nickel (Zhang et al., 2021).
- **Semiconductor manufacturing**: The production of CPUs and GPUs involves high-energy processes, such as silicon wafer production and photolithography (Chen & Lin, 2018).
- **Material sourcing**: Aluminum, rare earth elements, and plastics require substantial energy and generate emissions during extraction and refining (Wang et al., 2022).

While some studies cite lower values (e.g., 1,000 kg CO₂e for standard laptops), high-end models with advanced components (e.g., larger batteries, higher-performance GPUs) likely increase this figure. The exact value can vary based on manufacturing location, energy mix, and supply chain efficiency.
