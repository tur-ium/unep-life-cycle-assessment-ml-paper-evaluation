ReCiPe 2016 and CML-IA are both life cycle impact assessment (LCIA) methods primarily designed to evaluate environmental impacts, not social impacts. They focus on categories such as climate change, resource depletion, ecotoxicity, and human toxicity.

For a Social Life Cycle Assessment (SLCA), you would need methodologies specifically tailored to assess social and socio-economic aspects throughout the life cycle of a product or service. Some frameworks and guidelines for SLCA include:

1. **UNEP/SETAC Guidelines for SLCA**: These provide a comprehensive framework for conducting SLCA, focusing on social impacts across different stakeholder groups.

2. **Social Hotspots Database (SHDB)**: This tool helps identify potential social risks in supply chains and can be used alongside SLCA methodologies.

3. **Benoît et al.'s Framework**: This is one of the earlier frameworks for conducting SLCA, focusing on identifying relevant social impact categories and indicators.

4. **Product Social Impact Life Cycle Assessment (PSILCA)**: A database that provides data for conducting SLCA by assessing various social impacts across different life cycle stages.

While ReCiPe 2016 and CML-IA are valuable for environmental assessments, they do not cover the social dimensions required for a comprehensive SLCA. For social aspects, you should consider using or adapting frameworks specifically designed for SLCA.
