Here's a structured recommendation for the energy company, addressing each scenario:

**Overall Recommendation:** Employ a hybrid approach, starting with attributional LCA for initial understanding and transitioning to consequential LCA as deployment scales up.

**Scenario 1: Pilot-Scale Demonstration Phase**

*   **Goal & Scope:** Primarily **attributional LCA**. The goal is to quantify the environmental footprint of the pilot plant itself. The scope should include all direct inputs (electricity, water, materials) and outputs (hydrogen, waste) of the pilot plant.
*   **Data Collection:** Focus on primary data from the pilot plant operation. Use secondary data (e.g., ecoinvent) for upstream processes (e.g., electrode manufacturing).
*   **Justification:** At this stage, the pilot plant's impact on the broader system is negligible. Attributional LCA provides a clear baseline of the technology's inherent environmental performance (ISO 14040, 2006). Stakeholders (investors, regulators) are interested in the technology's potential, not its current system-wide effects.
*   **How LCA helps:** Attributional LCA identifies hotspots in the pilot plant's operation, guiding process optimization and material selection for future scale-up.

**Scenario 2: Large-Scale Commercial Deployment (Renewable Grid)**

*   **Goal & Scope:** Transition to **consequential LCA**. The goal is to assess the system-wide environmental impacts of increased hydrogen production in a renewable-dominated grid. The scope should include changes in electricity generation mix, potential displacement of other energy storage technologies, and impacts on grid infrastructure.
*   **Data Collection:** Model marginal electricity sources (e.g., battery storage, pumped hydro) that are displaced by hydrogen storage. Use market data and grid modeling to predict changes in electricity generation.
*   **Justification:** Large-scale deployment can influence the electricity market. Consequential LCA captures these indirect effects, providing a more realistic picture of the technology's overall impact (Ekvall & Weidema, 2004). Stakeholders (grid operators, policymakers) need to understand the system-wide consequences of hydrogen integration.
*   **How LCA helps:** Consequential LCA identifies potential unintended consequences, such as increased reliance on specific renewable energy sources or impacts on other energy storage technologies.

**Scenario 3: Large-Scale Deployment (Fossil-Based Grid)**

*   **Goal & Scope:** **Consequential LCA**, similar to Scenario 2, but with a focus on the fossil-based grid context. The goal is to assess the system-wide environmental impacts of increased hydrogen production in a fossil-dominated grid. The scope should include changes in electricity generation mix, potential displacement of other energy storage technologies, and impacts on grid infrastructure.
*   **Data Collection:** Model marginal electricity sources (e.g., natural gas peaker plants) that are displaced by hydrogen storage. Use market data and grid modeling to predict changes in electricity generation.
*   **Justification:** In a fossil-based grid, increased hydrogen production will likely drive increased fossil fuel consumption. Consequential LCA is crucial for quantifying this effect and comparing it to the baseline scenario (Zamagni et al., 2012). Stakeholders (environmental groups, policymakers) need to understand the impact on fossil resource use and global warming.
*   **How LCA helps:** Consequential LCA quantifies the net impact on fossil fuel consumption and greenhouse gas emissions, informing policy decisions and investment strategies.
