**Goal and Scope Definition for Life Cycle Assessment (LCA) of Land Use Options in a Rural Community**

### **Goal**  
The goal of this LCA is to evaluate and compare the environmental impacts, with a focus on greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions, of three land use scenarios for agricultural land in a rural community:  
1. **Conversion to a solar farm** (displacing agriculture),  
2. **Continued conventional agriculture**,  
3. **Agrivoltaics integration** (co-locating solar panels and agriculture).  

This assessment aims to provide evidence-based insights to stakeholders on the environmental trade-offs of each option, supporting informed decision-making aligned with climate change mitigation and local priorities.

---

### **Scope**  
#### **System Boundaries**  
- **Temporal**: 25-year lifespan (typical for solar infrastructure), with annual agricultural cycles included.  
- **Geographical**: Localized to the rural community’s agricultural land and regional energy/agricultural systems.  
- **Technical**:  
  - **Solar Farm**: Manufacturing, transportation, installation, operation, and decommissioning of panels/infrastructure.  
  - **Agriculture**: Inputs (fertilizers, pesticides, machinery), soil management, crop production, and associated GHG emissions (e.g., N₂O from fertilizers, CH₄ from livestock if applicable).  
  - **Agrivoltaics**: Combined solar infrastructure and modified agricultural practices, including potential yield changes and resource synergies.  

#### **Functional Unit**  
- **1 hectare of land over 25 years**, enabling direct comparison of land-use efficiency and impacts.  
- **Secondary units**:  
  - Energy output (kWh) for solar/agrivoltaics.  
  - Agricultural yield (tonnes of crop) for baseline and agrivoltaics.  

#### **Impact Categories**  
1. **Climate Change** (CO₂, CH₄, N₂O emissions).  
2. **Land Use Change** (biodiversity, soil health).  
3. **Resource Depletion** (water, minerals for solar panels).  
4. **Eutrophication/Acidification** (from agricultural runoff).  

#### **Key Assumptions**  
- **Energy Displacement**: Solar electricity replaces regional fossil fuel-based grid energy.  
- **Agricultural Practices**: Current practices remain constant unless altered by agrivoltaics (e.g., reduced yields but lower irrigation needs).  
- **Indirect Effects**: Excluded unless data permits (e.g., land-use displacement from agricultural loss).  

#### **Data Sources**  
- Solar panel lifecycle data (manufacturing emissions, efficiency).  
- USDA agricultural emissions factors (fertilizer use, machinery).  
- Peer-reviewed studies on agrivoltaics (Dinesh and Pearce, 2016).  

#### **Limitations**  
- Agrivoltaics data may be limited or context-specific.  
- Social/economic factors (jobs, tax revenue) are excluded but could complement the LCA in a broader decision framework.  

---

### **Justification**  
1. **Alignment with Stakeholder Priorities**: Focuses on GHG emissions (developer’s key argument) while addressing land-use trade-offs (community concerns).  
2. **Functional Unit Choice**: Balances direct land-use comparison (hectare-based) with output-specific metrics (kWh/tonnes) to clarify trade-offs.  
3. **System Boundaries**: Captures critical lifecycle stages (e.g., solar panel production, agricultural nitrous oxide) without overcomplicating the analysis.  
4. **Consequential Approach**: Implicitly considers avoided emissions from displacing fossil fuels, enhancing relevance to climate goals.  

This framework ensures the LCA provides actionable environmental insights while acknowledging the need for complementary socio-economic assessments to address all stakeholder interests.
