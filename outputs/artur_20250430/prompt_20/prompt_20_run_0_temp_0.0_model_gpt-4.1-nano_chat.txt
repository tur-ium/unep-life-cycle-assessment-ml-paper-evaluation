In an LCA study for oranges, it is important to account for the carbon stored in the trees because they act as carbon sinks during their growth phase. This stored carbon should be considered as a sequestration credit, reducing the overall greenhouse gas emissions associated with orange production. To handle this, you should include the carbon sequestration in the forest or orchard during the growth period as a negative emission in the life cycle inventory. This involves estimating the amount of biomass accumulated in the trees over time and converting it to stored carbon using appropriate biomass-to-carbon conversion factors. Additionally, it is crucial to consider the temporal aspect—carbon stored in trees is only sequestered during growth and may be released back into the atmosphere upon harvest, decomposition, or land-use change. Therefore, the storage should be modeled as a dynamic process, reflecting the growth cycle and lifespan of the orchard. Incorporating this sequestration aligns with best practices in LCA, providing a more accurate assessment of the climate impact by recognizing the role of trees as carbon reservoirs during their growth phase.
