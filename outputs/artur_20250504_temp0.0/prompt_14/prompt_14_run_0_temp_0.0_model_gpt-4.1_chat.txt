The carbon footprint of producing a high-end consumer laptop is typically in the range of **200–400 kg CO₂e** (0.2–0.4 tonnes CO₂e), with some estimates for premium models reaching up to **500 kg CO₂e** (0.5 tonnes CO₂e).

### Explanation and Sources

- **Apple MacBook Pro (2023):** Apple’s environmental reports state that the production of a 16-inch MacBook Pro (M2 Pro, 2023) results in approximately **340 kg CO₂e** (Apple 2023).
- **Dell XPS 13 (2022):** Dell’s Product Carbon Footprint report for the XPS 13 (9315) estimates **320 kg CO₂e** for manufacturing (Dell 2022).
- **Meta-analyses:** A review by Malmodin and Lundén (2018) found that the embodied emissions for a typical laptop are **200–400 kg CO₂e**, with higher-end models at the upper end due to more powerful components and larger batteries.
- **Lifecycle Assessments:** Teehan and Kandlikar (2013) estimate the manufacturing phase of a laptop accounts for **70–80%** of its total lifecycle emissions, with a typical value of **300 kg CO₂e** for production.

### Main Contributors

- **Integrated Circuits (CPU, GPU, RAM, SSD):** The most carbon-intensive components, due to energy-intensive semiconductor manufacturing.
- **Display:** Large, high-resolution screens increase emissions.
- **Battery:** Larger batteries in high-end models add to the footprint.
- **Materials and Assembly:** Aluminum chassis and complex assembly processes contribute additional emissions.
