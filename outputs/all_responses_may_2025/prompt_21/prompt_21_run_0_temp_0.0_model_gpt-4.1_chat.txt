A Life Cycle Assessment (LCA) comparing your product with an industry average product has several limitations. First, industry averages often mask significant variability in materials, manufacturing processes, and supply chains, making direct comparisons potentially misleading. Data quality and availability can differ between your product and the industry average, leading to inconsistencies in system boundaries, assumptions, and impact categories. Additionally, LCAs rely on numerous assumptions regarding product use, lifespan, and end-of-life scenarios, which may not be equivalent between the two products. Differences in functional performance or service provided can further complicate fair comparison. Finally, LCAs typically focus on quantifiable environmental impacts and may not capture social or economic factors, limiting the assessment’s comprehensiveness. These limitations mean that LCA results should be interpreted with caution and supplemented with additional context where possible.
