Here's a methodological strategy for an LCA comparing battery recycling and reuse in energy storage, addressing data uncertainty, technological variability, boundaries, functional unit, and stakeholder communication:

**Methodological Strategy**

This LCA will compare the environmental impacts of end-of-life (EOL) lithium-ion batteries (LIBs) managed through two scenarios: (1) recycling and (2) reuse in stationary energy storage systems (ESS). Due to limited publicly available LCI data for reuse, a hybrid approach combining process-based and economic input-output (EIO) LCA will be employed (Suh et al., 2004).

**Data Uncertainty and Technological Variability:**

Addressing data uncertainty is crucial. For recycling, we will use primary data where available (e.g., from published studies on specific recycling processes like hydrometallurgy or pyrometallurgy). When primary data is unavailable, secondary data from databases like Ecoinvent will be used, acknowledging its limitations (Wernet et al., 2016). Monte Carlo simulations will be performed to quantify the impact of parameter uncertainty on the LCA results (Heijungs & Suh, 2002).

Technological variability in recycling will be addressed by modeling multiple recycling pathways (e.g., hydrometallurgy, pyrometallurgy, direct recycling) as separate scenarios. For reuse, a scenario analysis will be conducted, considering different battery degradation rates, ESS application types (e.g., residential, grid-scale), and end-of-life management options for the reused batteries (e.g., eventual recycling). Sensitivity analysis will be used to assess the impact of key assumptions, such as battery lifespan in the ESS application, on the overall results. EIO-LCA will be used to estimate the impacts of refurbishment and integration of batteries into ESS, using economic data to represent the material and energy flows (Hendrickson et al., 2006).

**Boundaries and Functional Unit:**

The system boundary will encompass the entire life cycle, from battery collection at the EOL to the final disposition of materials or energy storage service. For recycling, this includes transportation, pre-processing, recycling processes, and waste management. For reuse, it includes transportation, testing, refurbishment, integration into the ESS, operation of the ESS, and eventual EOL management (recycling or disposal).

The functional unit will be "1 kWh of energy storage capacity provided over a defined period (e.g., 10 years)." This allows for a direct comparison of the environmental impacts associated with providing the same energy storage service through recycling and new battery production versus reuse.

**Justification to Stakeholders:**

To communicate the LCA methodology to stakeholders unfamiliar with LCA, we will emphasize the following:

*   **Transparency:** We will clearly document all data sources, assumptions, and limitations.
*   **Scenario Analysis:** We will present results for multiple scenarios to reflect the range of possible outcomes and technological variability.
*   **Sensitivity Analysis:** We will highlight the key parameters that significantly influence the results, allowing stakeholders to understand the robustness of the findings.
*   **Visualizations:** We will use clear and concise visualizations (e.g., bar charts, Sankey diagrams) to present the results and facilitate understanding.
*   **Plain Language:** We will avoid technical jargon and explain the LCA methodology in plain language. We will explain that LCA is a tool to compare the environmental burdens of different options, not to provide definitive answers, but to inform decision-making. We will emphasize that the results are based on the best available data and assumptions, and that further research is needed to refine the analysis.
