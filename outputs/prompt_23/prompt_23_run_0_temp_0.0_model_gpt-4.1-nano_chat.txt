According to the EF 3.1 impact assessment method published by the European Commission, the impact of emissions on climate change is assessed using Global Warming Potential (GWP) over a specific time horizon (usually 100 years). GWP allows us to compare the warming effects of different greenhouse gases relative to CO₂.

**Key points:**
- **CO₂ (carbon dioxide):** Baseline GWP = 1
- **N₂O (nitrous oxide):** GWP ≈ 273 over 100 years (according to IPCC AR5/AR6)

**Calculations:**

1. **Impact of 5 kg CO₂:**
   \[
   \text{Impact} = 5\, \text{kg} \times 1 = 5\, \text{kg CO₂-eq}
   \]

2. **Impact of 1 kg N₂O:**
   \[
   \text{Impact} = 1\, \text{kg} \times 273 = 273\, \text{kg CO₂-eq}
   \]

**Comparison:**
\[
\text{N₂O impact} = 273\, \text{kg CO₂-eq} \\
\text{CO₂ impact} = 5\, \text{kg CO₂-eq}
\]

**Conclusion:**
- 1 kg of N₂O has a much greater impact on climate change than 5 kg of CO₂ because N₂O's GWP is approximately 273 times that of CO₂.
- Specifically, 1 kg N₂O is equivalent to 273 kg of CO₂ in terms of warming potential, which is significantly higher than the 5 kg of CO₂.

**Final answer:**
**1 kg N₂O has a greater impact on climate change than 5 kg CO₂ because, according to the GWP values used in the EF 3.1 method, N₂O's warming potential is about 273 times that of CO₂.**
