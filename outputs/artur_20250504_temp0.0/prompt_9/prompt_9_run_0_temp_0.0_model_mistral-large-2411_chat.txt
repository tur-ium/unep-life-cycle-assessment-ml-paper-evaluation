To systematically compare the two conflicting Life Cycle Assessments (LCAs) of plant-based meat substitutes, follow this analytical framework:

1. **Methodological Comparison**:
   - **Data Sources**: Check if both studies use similar data sources for emissions, energy use, and agricultural practices. Differences in data sources can lead to varying results (Guinée et al., 2002).
   - **System Boundaries**: Compare the boundaries set for each LCA. Inconsistent boundaries, such as including or excluding certain life cycle stages (e.g., land use change, packaging), can significantly affect outcomes (ISO, 2006).
   - **Functional Units**: Ensure both studies use comparable functional units (e.g., per kg of protein, per serving). Different functional units can lead to misinterpretations (Klöpffer & Grahl, 2009).
   - **LCIA Methods**: Examine the Life Cycle Impact Assessment (LCIA) methods employed. Different methods (e.g., ReCiPe, CML) can yield different environmental impact results (Hauschild et al., 2013).

2. **Harmonization and Reliability Determination**:
   - **Sensitivity Analysis**: Conduct a sensitivity analysis to understand how variations in key parameters (e.g., emission factors, energy use) affect the results. This can help identify which study's conclusions are more robust.
   - **Data Quality Assessment**: Evaluate the quality and reliability of the data used in each study. Higher quality data generally leads to more reliable conclusions (Weidema et al., 2013).
   - **Peer Review and Validation**: Check if the studies have undergone peer review and if they have been validated by independent experts. This can provide additional credibility to the findings.

3. **Recommendations for Future LCAs**:
   - **Standardized Methodologies**: Adopt standardized methodologies and guidelines, such as those provided by the ISO 14040 series, to ensure consistency and comparability (ISO, 2006).
   - **Transparent Reporting**: Ensure transparent reporting of data sources, assumptions, and methodological choices to facilitate easier comparison and replication (Guinée et al., 2002).
   - **Collaborative Efforts**: Encourage collaborative efforts among researchers to share data and methodologies, reducing the likelihood of discrepancies (Klöpffer & Grahl, 2009).

By following this framework, it is possible to systematically compare the two LCAs, identify the sources of discrepancy, and determine which conclusions are more reliable. This approach also provides a roadmap for future LCAs to avoid similar issues.
