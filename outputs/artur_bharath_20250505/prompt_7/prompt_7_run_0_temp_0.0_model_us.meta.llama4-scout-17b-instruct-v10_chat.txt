Based on the scenarios provided, a structured recommendation for the selection of the appropriate goal and scope, as well as data collection methodology, is as follows:

**Scenario 1: Pilot-scale demonstration phase**

* Goal and scope: The primary goal is to evaluate the environmental performance of the novel hydrogen production process at a small scale. The scope should be limited to the production phase, focusing on the direct inputs and outputs of the process (e.g., electricity use, water consumption, and hydrogen production). 
* Methodological approach: An attributional life cycle assessment (LCA) approach is suitable for this scenario, as it provides a snapshot of the current environmental performance of the process (de Pauw et al., 2014). This approach will help identify areas for improvement and provide a baseline for future comparisons.
* Data collection: Primary data should be collected from the pilot-scale demonstration, including energy consumption, material inputs, and emissions. Secondary data can be used for background processes (e.g., electricity grid mix).

**Scenario 2: Large-scale commercial deployment in an electricity grid dominated by renewable energy**

* Goal and scope: The goal is to assess the consequential impacts of large-scale hydrogen production on the electricity grid and the environment. The scope should include the production phase, as well as the end-use phase (e.g., energy storage and grid interactions).
* Methodological approach: A consequential LCA approach is recommended, as it evaluates the potential consequences of changes in the system (e.g., increased hydrogen production) on the overall environmental performance (Weber et al., 2018). This approach will help understand the impact of hydrogen production on the grid and the environment.
* Data collection: A combination of primary and secondary data should be used. Primary data can be collected from the large-scale commercial deployment, while secondary data can be used for grid-specific information (e.g., renewable energy mix, grid capacity).

**Scenario 3: Large-scale deployment in a grid dominated by fossil-based electricity**

* Goal and scope: Similar to Scenario 2, the goal is to assess the consequential impacts of large-scale hydrogen production on the electricity grid and the environment. However, the scope should consider the specific characteristics of a fossil-based grid.
* Methodological approach: Again, a consequential LCA approach is recommended, as it can capture the potential consequences of changes in the system (e.g., increased hydrogen production) on the overall environmental performance (Weber et al., 2018). This approach will help understand the impact of hydrogen production on the grid and the environment in a fossil-based grid context.
* Data collection: A combination of primary and secondary data should be used. Primary data can be collected from the large-scale commercial deployment, while secondary data can be used for grid-specific information (e.g., fossil-based energy mix, grid capacity).

In all scenarios, both attributional and consequential LCA approaches can provide valuable insights. Attributional LCA can help evaluate the environmental performance of the hydrogen production process itself (de Pauw et al., 2014), while consequential LCA can assess the broader consequences of changes in the system (Weber et al., 2018). By using a combination of both approaches, the energy company can gain a comprehensive understanding of the life cycle impacts of hydrogen production and make informed decisions.

The choice of methodological approach and data collection methodology is crucial, as it directly affects the accuracy and relevance of the results. Stakeholders, including investors, policymakers, and the general public, will be interested in understanding the potential environmental consequences of the novel hydrogen production process. By using a structured and transparent approach, the energy company can ensure that the results are reliable and useful for decision-making.
