## Step 1: Understand the EF 3.1 impact assessment method
The EF 3.1 method is a life cycle impact assessment method developed by the European Commission. It assesses various environmental impacts, including climate change. To compare the climate change impact of different emissions, we need to look at their Global Warming Potentials (GWPs).

## Step 2: Identify the Global Warming Potentials (GWPs) of CO2 and N2O
CO2 (carbon dioxide) is the reference gas for GWPs, with a GWP of 1. N2O (nitrous oxide) has a much higher GWP. According to the IPCC (Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change) data used in many life cycle assessment methods, including EF 3.1, the GWP of N2O over a 100-year time horizon is 298 (for the IPCC Fourth Assessment Report) or 273 (for the IPCC Fifth Assessment Report, but the exact value can depend on the specific IPCC report referenced by EF 3.1).

## Step 3: Determine the GWP value used by EF 3.1 for N2O
The EF 3.1 method is based on the IPCC Fifth Assessment Report (AR5) for GWPs over a 100-year time horizon. According to AR5, the GWP of N2O is 265 (for a 100-year time horizon, but considering the direct GWP-100 value might slightly vary based on the exact source and rounding).

## Step 4: Calculate the climate change impact of 5 kg CO2
The climate change impact of CO2 is directly given by its mass because its GWP is 1. So, 5 kg CO2 = 5 kg CO2-eq.

## Step 5: Calculate the climate change impact of 1 kg N2O
Using the GWP of N2O from Step 3 (let's use 265 for simplicity, as it reflects a value that could be used in such assessments, though the exact value might depend on the specific reference), the climate change impact of 1 kg N2O = 1 kg * 265 = 265 kg CO2-eq.

## Step 6: Compare the climate change impacts
Now, compare the climate change impact of 5 kg CO2 (which is 5 kg CO2-eq) to that of 1 kg N2O (which is 265 kg CO2-eq). Clearly, 265 kg CO2-eq is greater than 5 kg CO2-eq.

## Step 7: Conclusion
1 kg N2O has a significantly larger impact on climate change than 5 kg CO2 because its GWP is much higher.

The final answer is: $\boxed{1 kg N2O}$
