Here's a framework for comparing conflicting LCAs of plant-based meat substitutes:

**1. Deconstruct and Compare Methodologies:**

*   **Data Sources:** Identify the origin of data for agricultural inputs (e.g., fertilizer use, irrigation), processing, transportation, and packaging. Differences in data quality and regional specificity (e.g., US vs. EU agriculture) can significantly impact results (Poore & Nemecek, 2018).
*   **System Boundaries:** Define the scope of each study. Does it include cradle-to-gate (farm to processing plant) or cradle-to-grave (including consumer use and end-of-life)? Broader boundaries capture more impacts but also introduce more uncertainty (Finnveden et al., 2009).
*   **Functional Unit:** Compare the functional unit used (e.g., 1 kg of protein, 1 kg of product, a serving). Inconsistent functional units make direct comparison impossible. Ensure the functional unit accurately reflects the intended function (e.g., nutritional equivalence) (ISO 14040, 2006).
*   **Life Cycle Impact Assessment (LCIA) Methods:** Identify the LCIA methods used (e.g., ReCiPe, CML). Different methods prioritize different environmental impacts and use different characterization factors, leading to varying results (Goedkoop et al., 2016).

**2. Harmonization and Reliability Assessment:**

*   **Sensitivity Analysis:** Examine the sensitivity analyses performed in each study. Identify key parameters driving the results and assess how changes in these parameters affect the conclusions.
*   **Data Quality Assessment:** Evaluate the data quality using established frameworks (e.g., pedigree matrix). Prioritize studies using more robust and representative data.
*   **Normalization and Weighting:** If possible, normalize the impact categories to a common reference point (e.g., per capita impact) to facilitate comparison. Be cautious about weighting, as it introduces subjective value judgments.
*   **Expert Consultation:** Consult with LCA experts to review the methodologies and data used in each study and identify potential biases or limitations.

**3. Recommendations for Future LCAs:**

*   **Standardized Methodologies:** Promote the use of standardized LCA methodologies and data sources for plant-based meat substitutes.
*   **Transparency and Data Sharing:** Encourage transparency in data reporting and data sharing to facilitate independent verification and comparison of results.
*   **Scenario Analysis:** Conduct scenario analyses to explore the impact of different agricultural practices, processing technologies, and consumer behaviors.
*   **Focus on Key Impact Categories:** Prioritize the assessment of key impact categories, such as greenhouse gas emissions, land use, and water use, which are most relevant to the environmental performance of plant-based meat substitutes.

By systematically comparing the methodologies, data, and assumptions used in each study, we can identify the sources of discrepancy and determine which conclusions are more reliable. This will help to inform decision-making and promote the development of more sustainable food systems.
