To address the energy company’s need to assess the life cycle impacts of hydrogen production via electrolysis, the choice between **attributional** and **consequential** LCA depends on the scenario’s scale, system boundaries, and stakeholder priorities.  

1. **Pilot-scale demonstration phase**:  
   - **Recommendation**: Use **attributional LCA** with narrow system boundaries.  
   - **Justification**: At this stage, the process is small-scale, and the focus is on direct resource use (e.g., electricity, water, materials) and emissions. Attributional LCA provides a clear, stable baseline for comparing the novel process to existing technologies, aligning with stakeholders’ need for immediate, measurable performance metrics (Guinée et al., 2002). Consequential LCA would introduce uncertainty due to speculative system-wide effects, which are irrelevant for a pilot project.  

2. **Large-scale commercial deployment in a renewable grid**:  
   - **Recommendation**: Use **consequential LCA** to evaluate system-level impacts.  
   - **Justification**: Renewable grids are dynamic, and large-scale hydrogen production could influence energy demand, grid stability, and renewable investment. Consequential LCA captures indirect effects, such as displacement of fossil fuels or changes in energy mix, critical for assessing how hydrogen integration affects fossil resource use and global warming (Huppes & Guinée, 2013). Stakeholders (e.g., grid operators) need this to optimize energy systems.  

3. **Large-scale deployment in a fossil-based grid**:  
   - **Recommendation**: Combine **attributional and consequential LCA**.  
   - **Justification**: Attributional LCA quantifies direct impacts (e.g., emissions from fossil electricity), while consequential LCA reveals how hydrogen production might drive grid transitions (e.g., reduced fossil reliance). This dual approach addresses stakeholder concerns about both immediate environmental burdens and long-term systemic shifts (Boulay et al., 2016).  

**Methodological Implications**:  
- **Attributional LCA** is suitable for scenarios with stable, well-defined system boundaries (e.g., pilot-scale) and when direct impacts are the priority.  
- **Consequential LCA** is essential for evaluating how hydrogen production interacts with broader energy systems, particularly in renewable or transitioning grids.  

By aligning methods with scenario-specific goals, the company can provide stakeholders with actionable insights into resource use, scarcity, and climate impacts.
