**Methodological Strategy**

This LCA will compare the environmental impacts of battery recycling versus battery reuse in energy storage applications. Given data limitations, a cradle-to-grave approach will be adopted.

*   **Functional Unit & System Boundaries:** The functional unit will be "providing 1 kWh of usable energy storage capacity over a 10-year lifespan." This allows for a fair comparison, accounting for differences in battery performance and longevity. The system boundaries will encompass:
    *   **Cradle-to-Gate (Battery Production):** Raw material extraction, processing, manufacturing of new batteries (for both recycling and reuse scenarios), and transportation to the point of use.
    *   **Use Phase:** Energy consumption during operation, including charging and discharging cycles, and any auxiliary energy needs.
    *   **End-of-Life:** Collection, transportation, and processing of batteries for either recycling or reuse. For reuse, this includes disassembly, testing, refurbishment, and reassembly. For recycling, this includes dismantling, pre-processing, and material recovery.
    *   **Gate-to-Gate (Recycling/Reuse):** The processes within the recycling or reuse facilities.

*   **Data Uncertainty & Technological Variability:**
    *   **Recycling:** LCI data for recycling will be sourced from the most recent peer-reviewed literature and publicly available databases (e.g., Ecoinvent, GaBi) (ISO 14044, 2006). Sensitivity analyses will be conducted to assess the impact of variations in recycling technology (e.g., hydrometallurgy vs. pyrometallurgy) and efficiency rates.
    *   **Reuse:** Since direct LCI data for reuse is limited, a hybrid approach will be employed. Process-based modeling will be used for well-defined processes (e.g., disassembly, transportation). For less-defined processes (e.g., refurbishment), we will use a combination of:
        *   **Expert Elicitation:** Gathering data from battery reuse experts to estimate energy consumption, material use, and waste generation.
        *   **Proxy Data:** Using data from similar industrial processes (e.g., electronics repair) and adjusting them based on expert knowledge.
    *   **Uncertainty Analysis:** Monte Carlo simulations will be performed to propagate uncertainties in input data (e.g., material composition, energy consumption) and assess the robustness of the results.

*   **Justification of Assumptions:** To communicate the methodology to stakeholders, we will:
    *   **Transparency:** Clearly document all assumptions, data sources, and limitations in a detailed report.
    *   **Sensitivity Analysis:** Present the results of sensitivity analyses to demonstrate the impact of key assumptions on the final results.
    *   **Visualizations:** Use clear and concise visualizations (e.g., flow diagrams, bar charts) to illustrate the system boundaries, data sources, and results.
    *   **Stakeholder Engagement:** Conduct workshops or presentations to explain the LCA methodology and solicit feedback from stakeholders. We will emphasize that LCA provides a comparative assessment, not an absolute measure, and that the results are based on the best available data and assumptions. We will also highlight the importance of continuous improvement and the potential for future refinements as data availability improves.

**References**
