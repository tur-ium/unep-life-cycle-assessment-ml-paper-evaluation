To systematically compare conflicting Life Cycle Assessments (LCAs) of plant-based meat substitutes, we must scrutinize methodological differences, harmonize findings, and provide recommendations for future studies.

**Methodological Differences**:

1. **Data Sources**: Variations in data quality and sources can significantly impact results. One study might use industry-reported data, while the other could rely on generic databases (Notarnicola et al., 2017).
   
2. **System Boundaries**: Differences in the scope of the study, such as cradle-to-grave versus cradle-to-gate, can lead to divergent conclusions. One LCA might include consumer-use impacts, while the other stops at retail (Heller et al., 2018).

3. **Functional Units**: The basis for comparison (e.g., per kilogram, per nutritional value) must be consistent. Discrepancies arise if one study uses mass while the other uses protein content (Smetana et al., 2019).

4. **LCIA Methods**: Different impact assessment methods (e.g., ReCiPe, TRACI) and versions can yield varying results. The choice of characterization factors for greenhouse gases or water use can also differ (Hauschild et al., 2018).

**Steps to Harmonize Findings**:

1. **Standardize Functional Units**: Convert all data to a common functional unit, such as per kilogram of protein or per serving, to ensure comparability.

2. **Align System Boundaries**: Expand or constrain the boundaries of both studies to match, ensuring all life cycle stages are consistently included or excluded.

3. **Cross-Verify Data Sources**: Validate the primary data sources and check for consistency. Use the same databases or correct for discrepancies in data quality.

4. **Apply Consistent LCIA Methods**: Recalculate impacts using the same LCIA method and factors. This can help identify if methodological choices are driving the differences.

**Determining Reliability**:

- **Sensitivity Analysis**: Conduct sensitivity analyses to understand how changes in key assumptions affect outcomes. This can reveal if the results are robust or highly sensitive to specific inputs (Björklund, 2002).

- **Peer Review and Transparency**: Evaluate the transparency and peer-review status of both studies. Studies with comprehensive documentation and peer validation are generally more reliable.

**Recommendations for Future LCAs**:

1. **Standardize Methodologies**: Adopt standardized guidelines for system boundaries, functional units, and impact assessment methods, such as those provided by the International Organization for Standardization (ISO 14040:2006).

2. **Transparent Reporting**: Ensure detailed reporting of data sources, assumptions, and methodological choices to facilitate reproducibility and comparison.

3. **Consistent Data Sources**: Use high-quality, consistent, and up-to-date data sources. Encourage the use of shared databases for key inputs like energy use and emissions factors.

By systematically addressing these aspects, we can better understand the discrepancies between LCAs and improve the reliability of future environmental assessments of plant-based meat substitutes.
