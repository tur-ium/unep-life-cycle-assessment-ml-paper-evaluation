You've hit on a common point of confusion! ReCiPe 2016 and CML-IA are **environmental** Life Cycle Impact Assessment (LCIA) methods, *not* Social Life Cycle Assessment (S-LCA) methods. They focus on environmental impacts like climate change, water depletion, and resource depletion.  While they are excellent for environmental LCAs, they **cannot** be directly used for S-LCA.

Here's a breakdown of why and what you *should* use for S-LCA:

**Why ReCiPe 2016 and CML-IA Don't Work for S-LCA:**

* **Different Scope:** Environmental LCIA focuses on physical flows of materials and energy and their impact on the environment. S-LCA focuses on the *social and socio-economic aspects* of a product's life cycle – how it affects people.  This includes things like working conditions, human rights, community impacts, and product responsibility.
* **Different Impact Categories:** ReCiPe and CML-IA have impact categories like Global Warming Potential, Ozone Depletion, etc. S-LCA has impact categories like Fair Wages, Health & Safety, Forced Labor, Community Involvement, etc.  They are fundamentally different.
* **Different Data Requirements:** Environmental LCIA needs data on material and energy inputs/outputs. S-LCA needs data on worker demographics, wages, working hours, health and safety incidents, community demographics, and more.
* **Different Calculation Methods:** The calculation methods are entirely different. Environmental LCIA uses characterization factors to convert emissions into impact scores. S-LCA uses different methodologies, often involving stakeholder engagement and qualitative assessments alongside quantitative data.



**What *to* Use for Social Life Cycle Assessment:**

There isn't *one* universally accepted method like ReCiPe for environmental LCA.  However, here are the most prominent and widely used methods for S-LCA:

* **SimaPro & Social Hotspot Database:** This is probably the most common approach. SimaPro is LCA software, and the Social Hotspot Database provides data and characterization factors for various social impact categories.  It's a quantitative approach.  It's not free, but it's well-supported and widely used.
* **PRIME (Product Responsibility Impact Assessment Method):**  Developed by Ecoinvent, PRIME is a method for assessing social and socio-economic impacts. It's often used in conjunction with other methods.
* **Eco-Social LCA (ESLCA):** This approach integrates environmental and social aspects into a single assessment. It's more holistic but can be complex.
* **Guidance from the United Nations Environment Programme (UNEP):**  UNEP has published guidelines for S-LCA that provide a framework for conducting assessments.  This is a good starting point for understanding the principles and process.
* **ISO 14044 (and related standards):** While ISO 14044 primarily focuses on environmental LCA, it provides a general framework for LCA that can be adapted for S-LCA.  ISO 14045 specifically addresses requirements for S-LCA.
* **Specific Sector/Issue-Based Methods:**  For certain industries (e.g., textiles, agriculture), there may be specific S-LCA methods tailored to the unique social risks and impacts of that sector.



**Key Considerations for S-LCA:**

* **Stakeholder Engagement:**  S-LCA *requires* engaging with stakeholders (workers, communities, NGOs) to understand the social impacts and prioritize issues.
* **Qualitative and Quantitative Data:**  S-LCA often involves a mix of quantitative data (e.g., wages, working hours) and qualitative data (e.g., interviews, surveys).
* **Context Specificity:** Social impacts are highly context-specific.  An impact that is significant in one region may not be in another.
* **Data Availability:**  Social data can be more difficult to obtain than environmental data.



**In summary:**  ReCiPe 2016 and CML-IA are excellent for environmental LCA, but they are *not* suitable for S-LCA. You need to use a dedicated S-LCA method and framework, such as those listed above.
