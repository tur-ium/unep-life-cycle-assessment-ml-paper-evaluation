# Multi-Output Allocation Method for Slaughter Process

For a slaughter process, economic allocation is typically the most appropriate multi-output allocation method. This approach distributes environmental impacts and resource use among multiple co-products (meat, hides, offal, etc.) based on their relative economic value.

Economic allocation is preferred for slaughter processes because:

1. It reflects the primary driver of the production system (economic value)
2. It aligns with the reality that the main purpose of animal production is to generate economically valuable products
3. It provides a stable allocation basis compared to physical properties, as market values tend to be more consistent than mass or energy content for slaughter co-products
4. It appropriately assigns lower impacts to by-products with lower economic value

While mass-based allocation could be used, it would inappropriately assign equal importance to high-value cuts and low-value by-products. System expansion (substitution) is often impractical due to the difficulty in identifying suitable substitutes for all slaughter co-products.

Economic allocation ensures that environmental burdens are distributed in proportion to the economic motivation driving the production system.
