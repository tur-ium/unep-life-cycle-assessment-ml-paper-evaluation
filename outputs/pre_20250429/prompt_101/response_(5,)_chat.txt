To address the methodological uncertainty between attributional and consequential life cycle assessment (LCA) approaches for the novel hydrogen production process using solar electrolysis, a structured recommendation is provided to guide the selection of the appropriate goal and scope for each scenario.

**Scenario 1: Pilot-scale demonstration phase**

* Goal: Consequential LCA
* Scope: Process-based, including hydrogen production, transportation, storage, and utilization
* Rationale:
Consequential LCA is suitable for the pilot-scale demonstration phase as it provides a comprehensive assessment of the overall environmental impacts of the process, including indirect land-use changes (ILUC) associated with primary energy sources. This approach allows for a detailed evaluation of the process's potential environmental consequences, which are critical during the demonstration phase when the technology's performance and efficiency are being fine-tuned.
* Citation: Fritsch et al. (2019) [1] recommends using consequential LCA for early-stage assessments of new technologies.

**Scenario 2: Large-scale commercial deployment in an electricity grid dominated by renewable energy**

* Goal: Consequential LCA
* Scope: Process-based, including hydrogen production, transportation, storage, and utilization; also consider the entire value chain, including upstream activities (e.g., electrolyzer manufacturing)
* Rationale:
Consequential LCA is still suitable for this scenario as the grid's dominance by renewable energy reduces the ILUC concerns. The focus shifts to the overall environmental impacts of the hydrogen production process, transportation, and utilization. Additionally, considering the entire value chain allows for a more comprehensive assessment of the technology's environmental performance.
* Citation: IPCC (2014) [2] emphasizes the importance of consequential LCA for assessing the environmental consequences of new technologies.

**Scenario 3: Large-scale deployment in a grid dominated by fossil-based electricity**

* Goal: Attributional LCA
* Scope: Process-based, including hydrogen production and utilization; exclude transportation and storage
* Rationale:
Attributional LCA is more suitable for this scenario as the primary concern is the direct environmental impact of the hydrogen production process. The use of fossil-based electricity in the grid introduces significant ILUC concerns, which attributional LCA can better address by excluding indirect land-use changes.
* Citation: European Commission (2018) [3] recommends using attributional LCA for assessing the environmental performance of energy systems.

In conclusion, the choice of goal and scope depends on the specific context and stakeholder interests. Consequential LCA is suitable for scenarios where the technology's overall environmental impacts are being assessed, while attributional LCA is more appropriate when direct environmental impacts are the primary concern.
